SECTION: 4

HEADING: Section 5 amended (Interpretation)

In section 5(1), insert in their appropriate alphabetical order:
INSERT:
**AI disclosure** means notice identifying material as generated or altered using an artificial intelligence system.
**AI-generated material** means digital material generated wholly or substantially by an artificial intelligence system.
**AI-generated political content** means AI-generated or AI-assisted material used in an election advertisement.
**AI-assisted material** means material created by a person where an artificial intelligence system has materially contributed to its generation, alteration, translation, editing, targeting, or presentation.
**artificial intelligence system** means a system that generates, modifies, analyses, recommends, or processes content or information using artificial intelligence.
**automated political advertising** means election advertising generated, selected, personalised, delivered, or materially modified by an automated system.
**deepfake** means synthetic media realistically depicting a person saying or doing something they did not say or do.
**political chatbot** means an artificial intelligence system or automated service used to communicate with electors for an electoral purpose.
**synthetic media** means digitally generated or materially altered audio, video, images, or other media purporting to depict a real person, event, place, or communication.
ENDINSERT

SECTION: 10

HEADING: Section 139C replaced (Artificially generated election material)

Replace section 139C with:

INSERT:
SECTION: 139C
HEADING: AI-generated and synthetic election material
(1) A person who publishes an election advertisement containing AI-generated, AI-assisted, synthetic, or materially digitally altered material must ensure that the material is clearly identified in accordance with this Act.
(2) Subsection (1) applies to material consisting of—
(a) text;
(b) audio;
(c) video;
(d) photographic or other visual material;
(e) an image or representation of a person;
(f) a digitally generated or altered voice;
(g) an artificial or digitally generated person; or
(h) any combination of those forms of material.
(3) The disclosure required by subsection (1) must be reasonably visible or audible to an ordinary elector.
(4) A person must not knowingly publish synthetic material in an election advertisement in a manner that is reasonably likely to cause an elector to believe that—
(a) a person said or did something that the person did not say or do;
(b) an event occurred that did not occur;
(c) an image, recording, or other material is an authentic record of an event when it is not;
(d) a candidate, political party, or other person endorsed a statement or position that they did not endorse; or
(e) the material was created without the use of artificial intelligence when the person knows that representation to be false.
(5) Subsection (4) does not apply to material that is clearly identified as synthetic or digitally altered.
(6) This section does not apply to—
(a) genuine news reporting;
(b) genuine documentary material;
(c) academic research;
(d) educational material;
(e) satire;
(f) parody;
(g) artistic expression; or
(h) material prescribed by regulations,
unless the material is published as an election advertisement.
(7) A person who knowingly contravenes subsection (4) commits an illegal practice.
(8) The Commission may issue guidance concerning the application of this section.
ENDINSERT

SECTION: 11

HEADING: New sections 139D to 139F inserted

After section 139C, insert:

INSERT:
SECTION: 139D
HEADING: AI-generated textual election advertising
(1) A person must not publish an election advertisement containing text generated wholly or substantially by an artificial intelligence system unless the advertisement clearly identifies that the text was generated or materially produced using artificial intelligence.
(2) Subsection (1) does not apply where artificial intelligence is used only for—
(a) spelling or grammar correction;
(b) translation;
(c) transcription;
(d) formatting;
(e) accessibility purposes;
(f) correction of typographical errors; or
(g) other minor editorial assistance that does not materially affect the substance or meaning of the advertisement.
(3) For the purposes of subsection (1), text is materially produced using artificial intelligence if an artificial intelligence system has generated, rewritten, or substantially altered the words used to communicate the principal message of the advertisement.
(4) The disclosure required by subsection (1) must be—
(a) clearly visible in a written advertisement;
(b) clearly audible in an audio advertisement; or
(c) displayed in a manner reasonably likely to be noticed by an ordinary elector in any other form of advertisement.
(5) A person who knowingly publishes an advertisement in contravention of subsection (1) commits an illegal practice.
(6) The Commission may issue guidance concerning the application of this section to different forms of election advertising.

SECTION: 139E
HEADING: Political chatbots
(1) A person who operates an artificial intelligence system that communicates with electors for an electoral purpose must clearly identify the system as an automated system.
(2) An artificial intelligence system to which subsection (1) applies must not—
(a) represent itself as a human elector;
(b) represent itself as a candidate;
(c) represent itself as a political party officer or representative;
(d) represent itself as an officer or employee of the Commission;
(e) falsely represent that a communication originated from a candidate, political party, public authority, or other person; or
(f) conceal the identity of the person responsible for operating the system.
(3) A person who operates a political chatbot must ensure that the chatbot provides, on request, the name of the person or organisation responsible for its operation.
(4) Where a political chatbot generates substantive political or election-related content, the chatbot must inform the user that the response was generated by an artificial intelligence system.
(5) A person must not knowingly use an artificial intelligence system to generate or distribute communications designed to give the false impression that a significant number of independent electors support or oppose a candidate, political party, or election proposition.
(6) Subsection (5) does not apply merely because an artificial intelligence system is used to respond to individual communications or to provide information requested by an elector.
(7) A person who knowingly contravenes this section commits an illegal practice.
(8) The Commission may issue guidance concerning the operation of political chatbots and other automated conversational systems.

SECTION: 139F
HEADING: Automated and personalised election advertising
(1) A promoter who uses an automated system to generate, select, modify, or deliver different versions of an election advertisement must retain records sufficient to identify—
(a) the system used;
(b) the principal criteria used to generate or select the advertisement;
(c) the categories of electors to whom the advertisement was directed;
(d) the number of materially different versions of the advertisement;
(e) the period during which the system was used; and
(f) the expenditure incurred in operating or using the system.
(2) A promoter must not use an artificial intelligence system to target election advertising on the basis of—
(a) an elector's inferred political opinion or political affiliation;
(b) an elector's inferred intention to vote for or against a candidate or political party;
(c) an inferred psychological characteristic or vulnerability;
(d) information obtained unlawfully; or
(e) information obtained through deception.
(3) A promoter must take reasonable steps to ensure that an automated system does not generate materially different election advertisements containing false information concerning—
(a) voting procedures;
(b) polling places;
(c) candidates;
(d) political parties;
(e) election dates;
(f) ballot papers; or
(g) the result of an election.
(4) The Commission may require a promoter to provide records relating to automated or personalised election advertising.
(5) A person who knowingly contravenes subsection (2) commits an illegal practice.
(6) The requirements of this section do not apply merely because an advertisement is displayed to an elector according to ordinary non-political technical criteria, including—
(a) language;
(b) device type;
(c) geographic availability;
(d) time of day; or
(e) accessibility requirements.
ENDINSERT

SECTION: 15

HEADING: Section 146JA replaced (Emergency removal of unlawful advertising)

Replace section 146JA with:

INSERT:
SECTION: 146JA
HEADING: Emergency removal of unlawful advertising
(1) If the Commission considers that an election advertisement presents an immediate and substantial risk of misleading electors about—
(a) voting procedures;
(b) the date or time of polling;
(c) the location of a polling place;
(d) the identity or actions of a candidate;
(e) the identity or position of a political party;
(f) the identity or actions of an electoral officer;
(g) a ballot paper or voting document;
(h) an election result; or
(i) another matter prescribed by regulations,
the Commission may direct the person responsible for the advertisement to remove it immediately.
(2) A direction under subsection (1) may be given where the misleading material is wholly or partly generated, altered, or distributed using artificial intelligence.
(3) A direction under subsection (1) must be confirmed by the High Court within 48 hours if the advertisement is to remain prohibited.
(4) If the Court does not confirm the direction within that period, the direction expires.
ENDINSERT

SECTION: 17

HEADING: Section 146JD replaced

Replace section 146JD with:

INSERT:
SECTION: 146JD
HEADING: Synthetic impersonation
(1) A person must not knowingly publish synthetic audio, video, photographic, textual, or other digital material that purports to show or represent—
(a) a candidate;
(b) a political party;
(c) a party leader;
(d) a Member of Parliament;
(e) a referendum promoter;
(f) an electoral officer;
(g) an officer or employee of the Commission; or
(h) another person prescribed by regulations,
making a statement, undertaking an action, expressing a view, giving an endorsement, withdrawing from an election, resigning, conceding an election, or giving a voting instruction that the person did not make, undertake, express, endorse, or give.
(2) Subsection (1) applies only where the material is reasonably likely to be understood by an ordinary elector as authentic.
(3) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an illegal practice.
(4) It is a defence if the person proves that—
(a) the material was clearly identified as synthetic;
(b) the material was published for genuine news reporting;
(c) the material was published for academic or educational research;
(d) the material was published for artistic, satirical, or parody purposes; or
(e) the person took reasonable steps to ensure that the material could not reasonably be understood as authentic.
ENDINSERT

SECTION: 18

HEADING: New sections 146JG to 146JI inserted

After sectin 146JD, insert:

INSERT:
SECTION: 146JG
HEADING: AI provenance records
(1) A promoter who publishes election advertising containing material to which section 139C applies must retain records sufficient to establish—
(a) the original material supplied to an artificial intelligence system, where applicable;
(b) the final material published;
(c) the artificial intelligence system used;
(d) the date on which the material was generated or modified;
(e) the person who authorised publication;
(f) the nature of the material generated or modified; and
(g) the principal instructions given to the artificial intelligence system where those instructions materially affected the published material.
(2) Records required by subsection (1) must be retained for at least 5 years after polling day.
(3) Subsection (1)(g) does not require a person to retain a complete record of an instruction where the instruction relates only to—
(a) spelling;
(b) grammar;
(c) formatting;
(d) translation;
(e) accessibility; or
(f) another minor editorial function.
(4) The Commission may require a person to produce records under this section for the purpose of investigating compliance with this Part.
(5) A person who knowingly destroys or falsifies a record required to be retained under this section commits an offence.

SECTION: 146JH
HEADING: Verification of synthetic material
(1) Where the Commission has reasonable grounds to believe that an election advertisement contains material represented as an authentic recording, image, document, statement, or other evidence, it may require the promoter to provide reasonable information concerning the authenticity of that material.
(2) A requirement under subsection (1) may include a requirement to provide—
(a) the original source material;
(b) information concerning the creation or modification of the material;
(c) information identifying whether artificial intelligence was used;
(d) records required under section 146JG; or
(e) other information reasonably necessary to establish whether the material is authentic.
(3) The Commission must exercise a power under this section only for the purpose of determining whether the material has been falsely represented as authentic.
(4) Nothing in this section authorises the Commission to determine whether a political opinion, interpretation, criticism, prediction, or policy claim is correct.
(5) A person must not knowingly provide false or misleading information to the Commission under this section.

SECTION: 146JI
HEADING: Deceptive use of artificial intelligence to impersonate persons or organisations
(1) A person must not knowingly use an artificial intelligence system to generate or materially alter election advertising for the purpose of impersonating another person or organisation.
(2) Subsection (1) applies where the impersonation is reasonably likely to cause an elector to believe that the communication originated from the person or organisation being impersonated.
(3) Without limiting subsection (1), a person must not use an artificial intelligence system to impersonate—
(a) a candidate;
(b) a political party;
(c) an electoral officer;
(d) the Commission;
(e) a government department or agency; or
(f) another person or organisation prescribed by regulations.
(4) A person who contravenes this section commits an illegal practice.
(5) This section does not apply to clearly identified satire, parody, artistic expression, academic research, or educational material that is not presented as authentic.
ENDINSERT
