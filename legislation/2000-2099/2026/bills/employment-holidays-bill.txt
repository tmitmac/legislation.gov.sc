COMMENTARYTITLE: As reported from the Education & Workforce Committee

COMMENTARYHEADING: Commentary

COMMENTARYSECTION: Recommendation

The Education & Workforce Committee has examined the Bill and recommends by majority that it be passed, with the amendments set out in this commentary.

The Committee considers that the Bill would make significant improvements to the operation of employment leave entitlements in Square Country by replacing the existing statutory framework with a clearer and more consistent system for annual holidays, public holidays, sick leave, bereavement leave, family violence leave, and other employment-related leave.

The Committee recognises that the Bill represents a substantial reform of employment law. While members have differing views about the appropriate balance between employee entitlements and obligations on employers, the majority considers that the existing framework has become unnecessarily complex and that legislative reform is required.

COMMENTARYSECTION: About the bill as introduced

The Employment Holidays Bill (the **Bill**) seeks to reform and consolidate the statutory framework governing holidays and leave entitlements for employees in Square Country.

The Bill is intended to provide employees and employers with a clearer understanding of their respective rights and obligations while reducing uncertainty around the calculation, administration, and payment of employment leave.

The principal purpose of the Bill is to establish a modern and more straightforward system for determining minimum employment leave entitlements. It would replace a number of existing rules and calculation methods with a more consistent statutory framework.

The Bill would provide for minimum entitlements relating to:

• **Annual holidays**, including the entitlement to paid annual leave and rules governing when and how annual holidays may be taken.

• **Public holidays**, including rules concerning an employee's entitlement to be absent from work on a public holiday and the circumstances in which an employee may be required to work.

• **Alternative holidays**, including circumstances in which an employee who works on a public holiday may receive an alternative day off.

• **Sick leave**, including the circumstances in which an employee may take paid sick leave and the evidence that may be required.

• **Bereavement leave**, providing employees with leave following the death of a close family member or another person where the statutory criteria are met.

• **Family violence leave**, providing protections for employees affected by family violence.

• **Parental and other statutory leave**, where those entitlements interact with the wider holidays framework.

A central feature of the Bill is the simplification of holiday and leave calculations.

The existing framework can require employers to apply different calculations depending on the type of leave being taken, an employee's work pattern, and the nature of their remuneration. The Bill seeks to reduce this complexity by establishing clearer statutory definitions and calculation methods.

The Bill would also provide rules for employees whose working hours or patterns are irregular. These provisions are intended to ensure that employees working part-time, variable, casual, or non-standard hours are able to determine their entitlements without requiring employers to rely on unnecessarily complicated calculations.

The Bill would establish rules for the treatment of ordinary earnings and other payments when calculating holiday and leave payments. The Committee considers that greater clarity in this area is particularly important because disputes over holiday pay can result in significant financial consequences for both employees and employers.

The Bill would also clarify the circumstances in which an employee and employer may agree that annual holidays will be taken at particular times. It would preserve the principle that employees are entitled to take their minimum statutory holidays and that employers cannot contract out of minimum employment standards.

The Bill would provide employers with greater flexibility to manage leave where there are legitimate operational requirements, while retaining statutory protections for employees.

The Bill also addresses the treatment of public holidays.

It would establish a clearer framework for determining whether a public holiday falls on a day that would otherwise be a working day for an employee. This is particularly relevant for employees who work rotating rosters, irregular hours, or non-standard working weeks.

Where an employee works on a public holiday, the Bill would provide for the applicable payment and alternative holiday arrangements. The Committee considers that these provisions should provide greater certainty for employers while preserving the principle that employees who work on public holidays should receive the statutory benefits associated with doing so.

The Bill further provides for record-keeping requirements.

Employers would be required to maintain sufficient records to demonstrate employees' hours, leave entitlements, leave taken, and payments made under the Act. These requirements are intended to support compliance and allow employees and enforcement authorities to establish whether statutory entitlements have been correctly provided.

The Bill would also contain enforcement provisions.

Employees would retain avenues for raising employment disputes where they believe their statutory entitlements have not been provided. Employers who fail to comply with the Act could be subject to enforcement action and applicable penalties.

The Committee considers that effective enforcement is necessary because minimum employment standards are intended to apply universally and should not depend solely on an employee's ability to negotiate with their employer.

The Bill would also include transitional provisions to manage the change from the existing holidays framework to the new system.

These provisions are particularly important because employees may have accrued leave under the existing legislation before the new Act comes into force. The Committee has therefore considered whether employees could lose accrued entitlements as a consequence of the reform.

The Committee considers that accrued minimum entitlements should be protected during the transition and that employers should be given sufficient time and guidance to update payroll and employment systems.

Overall, the Bill seeks to create a more comprehensible and administratively workable holidays system while maintaining minimum statutory protections for employees.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Legislative scrutiny

The Committee has undertaken detailed legislative scrutiny of the Bill because of the breadth of the proposed changes and their potential effect on almost every employment relationship in Square Country.

The Committee's consideration focused particularly on whether the Bill would produce a simpler and more predictable system without reducing the minimum protections currently available to employees.

Key matters considered include:

• **Annual holidays:** Whether the proposed entitlement and accrual arrangements provide employees with a meaningful right to paid annual leave.

• **Holiday pay:** Whether the proposed calculation methods are sufficiently clear and accurately reflect the employee's ordinary earnings.

• **Public holidays:** Whether the Bill appropriately determines when an employee is entitled to a public holiday and how work performed on public holidays should be compensated.

• **Alternative holidays:** Whether employees who work on public holidays receive an appropriate alternative entitlement.

• **Sick leave:** Whether the proposed entitlement provides adequate protection for employees while remaining workable for employers.

• **Bereavement leave:** Whether the statutory framework appropriately recognises different family and personal circumstances.

• **Family violence leave:** Whether the Bill provides sufficient protection for employees affected by family violence.

• **Irregular and variable work:** Whether employees with non-standard working arrangements can readily determine their entitlements.

• **Casual employees:** Whether the Bill provides appropriate treatment for employees whose hours and working patterns vary significantly.

• **Record keeping:** Whether employers have sufficient obligations to demonstrate compliance with the Act.

• **Enforcement:** Whether employees have effective avenues for recovering unpaid or incorrectly calculated entitlements.

• **Transitional arrangements:** Whether employees' existing entitlements are adequately protected when the new framework comes into force.

• **Employer compliance costs:** Whether the proposed framework can reasonably be implemented by businesses of different sizes.

• **Interaction with employment agreements:** Whether employers and employees can negotiate additional benefits while retaining the statutory minimum standards established by the Bill.

The Committee received submissions from employees, employers, businesses, unions, professional organisations, and other interested parties.

A number of submitters supported the objective of simplifying the Holidays framework but expressed concern about the transition to the new system.

Other submitters argued that the Bill should go further by increasing minimum leave entitlements, while some employers argued that the proposed framework should provide greater flexibility and reduce compliance costs.

The Committee has considered these competing views and has recommended amendments intended to improve the clarity and operation of the Bill.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Our process and proposed amendments

The Committee has undertaken its consideration of the Bill through written submissions, oral evidence, departmental advice, and detailed examination of the Bill's individual provisions.

Given the significance of the Bill, the Committee has not treated the legislation as a simple technical amendment. Members have considered the practical consequences for employees, employers, payroll systems, small businesses, and the wider employment system.

The Committee proposes amendments in several key areas.

**Clarification of holiday calculations**

The Committee recommends amendments to clarify the calculation of annual holiday payments and other leave payments. The intention is to reduce uncertainty and ensure that employees receive the amount to which they are legally entitled.

**Protection of accrued entitlements**

The Committee recommends strengthening the transitional provisions so that employees do not lose accrued statutory entitlements solely because the legislative framework changes.

**Public holiday arrangements**

The Committee recommends amendments clarifying how public holidays apply to employees working irregular or rotating schedules.

**Alternative holidays**

The Committee recommends clearer provisions concerning when an alternative holiday arises and when it may be taken.

**Sick leave evidence**

The Committee recommends clarifying when an employer may request medical evidence and who is responsible for any associated costs.

**Record keeping**

The Committee recommends clearer requirements for employers to maintain records relating to hours worked, leave taken, and payments made.

**Small-business implementation**

The Committee recommends transitional and guidance provisions to assist smaller employers in moving to the new framework without compromising employees' statutory rights.

**Drafting and consequential amendments**

The Committee recommends a number of technical amendments to improve consistency with other employment legislation and correct drafting, cross-referencing, and consequential issues identified during scrutiny.

The Committee considers that these amendments would improve the workability of the Bill without altering its fundamental purpose.

**Repeals**

The Committee recommends that the *Public Holidays Act 1988* be repealed, as there will be inconsitencies between public holiday entitlements set out in this bill and the *Public Holidays Act 1988*.

Consequentially, the Committee recommends including provisions relating to the administration of public holidays in this bill instead, i.e., listing all national public holidays, regional anniversaries, etc.


COMMENTARYSECTION: Freedoms Party differing view

The Freedoms Party does not support the Bill in its present form.

We believe the Government has approached this legislation from the wrong direction. Instead of treating paid holidays and leave as fundamental employment protections, the Bill places too much emphasis on simplifying the system for employers while failing to provide a sufficiently ambitious improvement in the rights of working people.

The existing Holidays framework is undoubtedly complicated. However, complexity is not in itself a reason to reduce or restrict an employee's entitlement. Where the existing system is difficult to administer, the answer should be to improve the law and provide employers with better guidance—not to make employees carry the cost of reform.

The Freedoms Party is particularly concerned about the treatment of annual holidays.

Employees should have a clear and meaningful right to take paid time away from work. We are concerned that the proposed framework could create circumstances where employees have less certainty about when leave can be taken or how much they are entitled to receive.

We are also concerned about the treatment of workers with irregular hours.

Many workers do not work a conventional Monday-to-Friday week. Rosters, variable hours, shift work, and casual employment are increasingly common. The law must ensure that these workers are not disadvantaged simply because their working arrangements are different.

The Freedoms Party also considers that the Bill does not go far enough in strengthening sick leave and family-related leave.

People should not be forced to choose between attending work while sick and losing income. Nor should employees facing serious family circumstances have to negotiate basic dignity and security with their employer.

We therefore consider that the Bill should have been accompanied by a broader increase in minimum leave entitlements.

The Freedoms Party also opposes provisions that we believe give employers excessive flexibility in determining when employees can take leave.

Employment is not an equal bargaining relationship in every circumstance. An employee may technically have a right under an employment agreement while lacking the practical ability to exercise that right.

For these reasons, the Freedoms Party does not support the Bill.

We believe a substantially stronger employment holidays framework is required—one that places the wellbeing, security, and rights of working people at its centre.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Square Country Greens differing view

The Square Country Greens support the objective of reforming the holidays system but do not support the Bill in its present form.

We believe the Bill represents a missed opportunity to establish a genuinely modern employment leave framework for Square Country.

The Greens agree that the existing system can be unnecessarily complicated. However, simplification must not become a justification for weakening employee protections.

We are particularly concerned that the Bill treats leave primarily as an administrative issue rather than recognising its importance to workers' health, family life, and wellbeing.

The Greens believe employees should have a stronger statutory entitlement to paid annual leave, sick leave, and family-related leave.

We are also concerned about the position of workers in insecure employment.

Casual workers, workers on variable rosters, and people employed across multiple workplaces can experience particular difficulty exercising their leave entitlements. A system that works well for a permanent full-time employee may not work equally well for someone whose hours change from week to week.

The Greens therefore believe that the Bill should contain stronger protections against employees losing practical access to their statutory entitlements because of their employment arrangements.

We also believe that family violence leave should be treated as a fundamental employment protection. Employees experiencing family violence may require flexibility, time away from work, and financial security. The legislation should make it as easy as reasonably possible for those employees to access their entitlements without exposing them to unnecessary scrutiny.

The Greens further believe that the Bill should do more to address the environmental and social consequences of long working hours and inadequate access to rest.

Annual holidays are not simply a financial benefit. They provide employees with an opportunity to rest, spend time with family and friends, and participate in their communities.

We therefore believe the Bill should establish a stronger minimum standard and provide additional protections for vulnerable workers.

While we acknowledge the work undertaken by the Committee to improve the Bill, the Greens cannot support the legislation in its current form.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Innovation Party differing view

The Innovation Party opposes the Bill.

Our position differs from both the Freedoms Party and the Square Country Greens. We do not believe that the central problem with the existing holidays system is that employees have insufficient leave or that employers have excessive flexibility.

Instead, we believe the Bill is an unnecessarily large intervention in an area that could be addressed through a substantially simpler legislative framework.

The Bill attempts to rewrite a large part of the employment holidays system while introducing significant new compliance obligations for employers.

For businesses, particularly small and innovative businesses, changing payroll systems and employment processes can carry significant costs. Those costs ultimately have to be absorbed by businesses, employees, or consumers.

The Innovation Party supports clear minimum employment standards, but legislation should be proportionate, technologically neutral, and straightforward to administer.

We are also concerned that the Bill does not adequately account for modern workplaces.

Employment is changing rapidly. Flexible working arrangements, remote work, project-based employment, contracting, and variable hours are increasingly common.

Rather than creating another highly prescriptive framework, Parliament should establish clear minimum standards and allow employers and employees to agree on arrangements that suit their circumstances.

The Innovation Party is particularly concerned about the administrative complexity created by the Bill's record-keeping and calculation provisions.

We believe modern payroll technology should be used to simplify compliance rather than requiring employers to navigate increasingly detailed statutory rules.

The Innovation Party also considers that the Government has not adequately demonstrated why such a comprehensive rewrite is required at this time.

There are genuine problems with the existing system, but those problems could have been addressed through targeted amendments, improved guidance, and modernisation of payroll requirements.

We therefore believe the Bill is too broad, too prescriptive, and likely to impose unnecessary compliance costs.

The Innovation Party does not support the Bill and would instead support a narrower reform package focused on simplifying calculations, digitising employment records, and ensuring that existing minimum employee protections are properly enforced.

PARTNUMBER: 1

PARTTITLE: Preliminary provisions

SECTION: 1

HEADING: Title

1. This Act is the Employment Holidays Act 2026.

SECTION: 2

HEADING: Commencement

1. This Act comes into force on 1 January 2027.

2. The Minister may, by Order in Council, appoint different days for the commencement of different provisions of this Act.

3. An order made under subsection (2) may not appoint a day later than 1 January 2028.

SECTION: 3

HEADING: Purpose

The purposes of this Act are—

(a) to promote balance between work and other aspects of employees' lives; and

(b) to provide minimum entitlements to paid annual holidays; and

(c) to provide minimum entitlements to public holidays; and

(d) to provide minimum entitlements to alternative holidays where employees work on public holidays; and

(e) to provide minimum entitlements to paid sick leave; and

(f) to provide minimum entitlements to bereavement leave; and

(g) to provide minimum entitlements to family violence leave; and

(h) to ensure that employees receive the correct payments for holidays and leave; and

(i) to establish clear requirements for employers to keep employment records; and

(j) to provide effective mechanisms for enforcing minimum holiday and leave entitlements.

SECTION: 4

HEADING: Overview

1. This Act establishes minimum employment entitlements relating to holidays and leave.

2. Part 1 contains preliminary provisions.

3. Part 2 deals with annual holidays.

4. Part 3 deals with public holidays.

5. Part 4 deals with alternative holidays.

6. Part 5 deals with sick leave.

7. Part 6 deals with bereavement leave.

8. Part 7 deals with family violence leave.

9. Part 8 deals with parental and other forms of leave.

10. Part 9 deals with payment calculations.

11. Part 10 deals with employment records.

12. Part 11 deals with enforcement and disputes.

13. Part 12 deals with offences and penalties.

14. Part 13 deals with special employment circumstances.

15. Part 14 deals with annual closedowns.

16. Part 15 deals with public holiday arrangements.

17. Part 16 deals with administration and powers.

18. Part 17 deals with regulations.

19. Part 18 contains transitional and miscellaneous provisions.

SECTION: 5

HEADING: Interpretation

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires—

“annual holiday” means a paid holiday to which an employee becomes entitled under Part 2:

“average daily pay” means the amount determined under [[section:145]]:

“average weekly earnings” means the amount determined under [[section:143]]:

“Commission” means the Employment Relations Commission:

“day” means a calendar day:

“employee” has the meaning given to that term by the Employment Relations Act:

“employer” has the meaning given to that term by the Employment Relations Act:

“employment agreement” means an individual or collective employment agreement:

“family violence” has the meaning given to that term by the Family Violence Act:

“family violence leave” means leave taken under Part 7:

“leave” includes annual holidays, alternative holidays, sick leave, bereavement leave, family violence leave, parental leave, and any other leave provided under this Act:

“ordinary weekly pay” means the amount determined under [[section:142]]:

“otherwise working day” means a day that would otherwise be a working day for the employee:

“public holiday” means a day declared to be a public holiday under this Act:

“relevant daily pay” means the amount determined under [[section:140]]:

“working day” means a day on which an employee would otherwise be expected to work for the employer.

SECTION: 6

HEADING: Act binds the Crown

This Act binds the Crown.

SECTION: 7

HEADING: Minimum entitlements

1. An employment agreement must not provide an employee with an entitlement that is less favourable than an entitlement provided by this Act.

2. An employer and employee may agree to provide an entitlement more favourable than the minimum entitlement under this Act.

3. A provision in an employment agreement that purports to exclude or reduce a minimum entitlement under this Act is void to the extent of the exclusion or reduction.

SECTION: 8

HEADING: Contracting out prohibited

1. An employee must not agree to waive an entitlement under this Act.

2. An employer must not require or induce an employee to agree to waive an entitlement under this Act.

3. An agreement contrary to this section has no effect.

SECTION: 9

HEADING: Relationship with other legislation

1. This Act must be read together with the Employment Relations Act and other employment legislation.

2. If another enactment provides a more favourable employment entitlement than this Act, the more favourable entitlement applies.

SECTION: 10

HEADING: Employment agreements

1. Every employer must ensure that the employee's employment agreement identifies the employee's minimum entitlements under this Act.

2. An employer must provide an employee with information about the employee's holiday and leave entitlements on request.

SECTION: 11

HEADING: Prohibition on discrimination

An employer must not disadvantage an employee because the employee—

(a) exercises a right under this Act;

(b) requests leave under this Act;

(c) takes leave under this Act; or

(d) makes a complaint concerning a breach of this Act.

PARTNUMBER: 2

PARTTITLE: Annual holidays

SECTION: 12

HEADING: Entitlement to annual holidays

1. An employee is entitled to at least 4 weeks' paid annual holidays for each completed year of employment.

2. An employer may provide an employee with more than 4 weeks' annual holidays.

3. An additional contractual entitlement must be recorded in the employment agreement.

SECTION: 13

HEADING: When annual holiday entitlement arises

1. An employee becomes entitled to annual holidays on completing 12 months' continuous employment with the employer.

2. Each subsequent entitlement arises on the anniversary of the date on which the employee became entitled to the previous entitlement.

SECTION: 14

HEADING: Continuous employment

For the purposes of determining annual holiday entitlement, employment is continuous despite—

(a) an employee taking leave under this Act;

(b) an employee taking parental leave;

(c) a temporary cessation of work;

(d) a transfer of employment where continuity is preserved by law; or

(e) any other circumstance prescribed by regulation.

SECTION: 15

HEADING: Amount of annual holidays

1. An employee's annual holiday entitlement is 4 weeks for every completed year of employment.

2. An employer may agree to provide annual holidays in excess of the minimum entitlement.

SECTION: 16

HEADING: Taking annual holidays

1. An employer and employee must try to agree on when annual holidays are to be taken.

2. An employer must not unreasonably refuse an employee's request to take annual holidays.

3. Annual holidays may be taken in periods agreed between the employer and employee.

SECTION: 17

HEADING: Employer may require annual holidays

1. An employer may require an employee to take annual holidays if—

(a) the employer and employee cannot agree on when the holidays are to be taken; and

(b) the employer has given at least 14 days' notice.

2. An employer must not require an employee to take annual holidays at a time that would unfairly disadvantage the employee.

SECTION: 18

HEADING: Division of annual holidays

1. Annual holidays may be taken in separate periods.

2. An employee may take annual holidays in periods of less than 1 week if the employer agrees.

3. An employer must allow an employee to take at least 2 weeks of annual holidays continuously if the employee requests it.

SECTION: 19

HEADING: Advance annual holidays

1. An employer may agree to allow an employee to take annual holidays before the entitlement has arisen.

2. Annual holidays taken in advance may be deducted from the employee's subsequent entitlement.

3. An employer must not require an employee to take annual holidays in advance.

SECTION: 20

HEADING: Annual holidays during employment

Annual holidays must not be treated as sick leave, bereavement leave, or family violence leave unless this Act expressly provides otherwise.

SECTION: 21

HEADING: Carry-over of annual holidays

1. Unused annual holidays remain available to the employee until taken or otherwise dealt with under this Act.

2. An employer must not cancel annual holidays merely because the employee has failed to take them within a particular year.

SECTION: 22

HEADING: Cashing up annual holidays

1. An employee may request payment instead of taking up to 1 week of annual holidays in each entitlement year.

2. A request under subsection (1) must be made in writing.

3. An employer may agree to the request but is not required to do so.

4. An employer must not pressure or encourage an employee to cash up annual holidays.

SECTION: 23

HEADING: Payment of cashed-up holidays

1. If an employer agrees to cash up annual holidays, the employer must pay the employee the amount agreed before the holidays would otherwise have been taken.

2. The payment must not be less than the amount the employee would have received under Part 9.

SECTION: 24

HEADING: Cancellation of annual holidays

1. An employer must not cancel annual holidays after they have commenced except where reasonably necessary.

2. If an employer cancels annual holidays, the employer must reimburse the employee for reasonable non-refundable costs caused by the cancellation.

SECTION: 25

HEADING: Employee cancellation

1. An employee may request cancellation of annual holidays before the holidays begin.

2. The employer may agree to the request.

SECTION: 26

HEADING: Annual holidays during sickness

1. If an employee becomes sick during annual holidays, the employee may request that the affected period be treated as sick leave.

2. An employer must approve the request if the employee satisfies the requirements for sick leave.

SECTION: 27

HEADING: Annual holidays during bereavement

1. If an employee suffers a bereavement during annual holidays, the affected period must be treated as bereavement leave if the employee is entitled to bereavement leave.

2. Annual holidays must not be deducted for the period treated as bereavement leave.

SECTION: 28

HEADING: Annual holidays during family violence leave

1. If an employee becomes entitled to family violence leave during annual holidays, the employee may take family violence leave instead of annual holidays.

2. Annual holidays must not be deducted for the period treated as family violence leave.

SECTION: 29

HEADING: Annual holidays and public holidays

1. A public holiday occurring during annual holidays must not be deducted from the employee's annual holiday entitlement if the day would otherwise be a working day.

2. The employee must receive the public holiday entitlement provided by Part 3.

SECTION: 30

HEADING: Annual holiday entitlement on termination

1. When employment ends, the employer must calculate the employee's outstanding annual holiday entitlement.

2. The employer must pay the employee all annual holiday pay owing at the end of employment.

SECTION: 31

HEADING: Payment of outstanding holidays

Outstanding annual holiday pay must be paid no later than the employee's final payday unless another time is authorised by regulation.

PARTNUMBER: 3

PARTTITLE: Public holidays

SECTION: 32

HEADING: Public holidays

The following days are public holidays in Square Country—

(a) New Year's Day;

(b) the day after New Year's Day;

(c) National Day;

(d) Good Friday;

(e) Easter Monday;

(f) King's Birthday;

(g) Matariki;

(h) Labour Day;

(i) Christmas Day;

(j) Boxing Day; and

(k) any other day declared by Act or Order in Council to be a public holiday.

SECTION: 33

HEADING: Public holiday falling on weekend

1. If Christmas Day, Boxing Day, New Year's Day, or the day after New Year's Day falls on a Saturday or Sunday, the public holiday must be observed on the next applicable working day.

2. Regulations may provide for the observance of other public holidays that fall on weekends.

SECTION: 34

HEADING: Entitlement to public holiday

1. An employee is entitled to a public holiday if the public holiday falls on an otherwise working day.

2. An employee is entitled to be absent from work on the public holiday unless required to work under section 36.

SECTION: 35

HEADING: Payment for public holiday not worked

1. If an employee does not work on a public holiday that is an otherwise working day, the employer must pay the employee the amount determined under Part 9.

2. No annual holiday entitlement may be deducted for the public holiday.

SECTION: 36

HEADING: Employee required to work on public holiday

An employer may require an employee to work on a public holiday only if—

(a) the employment agreement provides that the employee may be required to work on the public holiday; or

(b) the employee agrees to work on the public holiday.

SECTION: 37

HEADING: Payment for work on public holiday

1. An employee who works on a public holiday must be paid at least 1.5 times the employee's relevant daily pay for the time worked on the public holiday.

2. An employment agreement may provide for a higher rate.

SECTION: 38

HEADING: Alternative holiday

1. An employee who works on a public holiday that is an otherwise working day is entitled to an alternative holiday.

2. The alternative holiday must be a paid day off.

SECTION: 39

HEADING: Date of alternative holiday

1. The employer and employee must agree on when the alternative holiday is to be taken.

2. If no agreement is reached, the employer may designate a date by giving at least 14 days' notice.

3. The alternative holiday must be taken on a day that would otherwise be a working day for the employee.

SECTION: 40

HEADING: Transfer of public holiday

1. An employer and employee may agree to transfer a public holiday to another day.

2. A transferred public holiday must be treated as a public holiday for the purposes of this Act.

3. The agreement must be recorded in writing.

SECTION: 41

HEADING: Transfer restrictions

A public holiday must not be transferred if the transfer would—

(a) result in an employee losing a minimum entitlement;

(b) result in a public holiday being transferred to a day that is not a working day unless permitted by regulation; or

(c) otherwise contravene this Act.

SECTION: 42

HEADING: Alternative holiday for part-day work

1. If an employee works only part of a public holiday, the employee may be entitled to an alternative holiday if the employee would otherwise have worked the whole day.

2. Regulations may prescribe methods for calculating the entitlement.

SECTION: 43

HEADING: Sickness on public holiday

1. If an employee is unable to work on a public holiday because of sickness or injury, the day must be treated as a public holiday if it would otherwise have been a working day.

2. The day must not be deducted from the employee's sick leave entitlement.

SECTION: 44

HEADING: Bereavement on public holiday

1. If an employee suffers a bereavement on a public holiday, the day must be treated as a public holiday.

2. The day must not be deducted from the employee's bereavement leave entitlement.

SECTION: 45

HEADING: Family violence on public holiday

1. If an employee is unable to work on a public holiday because of circumstances giving rise to family violence leave, the day must continue to be treated as a public holiday.

2. The day must not be deducted from the employee's family violence leave entitlement.

SECTION: 46

HEADING: Public holiday during parental leave

1. A public holiday occurring during parental leave does not create an additional entitlement unless the employee would otherwise have worked on that day.

2. Regulations may provide for special circumstances.

SECTION: 47

HEADING: Public holiday falling during annual closedown

A public holiday occurring during an annual closedown must be treated as a public holiday and must not be deducted from annual holiday entitlement.

PARTNUMBER: 4

PARTTITLE: Alternative holidays

SECTION: 48

HEADING: Purpose of alternative holidays

The purpose of this Part is to provide employees who work on public holidays with paid time away from work.

SECTION: 49

HEADING: Entitlement to alternative holiday

1. An employee is entitled to an alternative holiday if—

(a) the employee works on a public holiday; and

(b) the public holiday is an otherwise working day.

2. The entitlement arises immediately after the public holiday is worked.

SECTION: 50

HEADING: Alternative holiday must be paid

An alternative holiday must be paid at the employee's relevant daily pay or average daily pay, as applicable.

SECTION: 51

HEADING: Taking alternative holiday

An employee may take an alternative holiday on any day agreed with the employer.

SECTION: 52

HEADING: Employer may designate day

1. If an employer and employee cannot agree on the date of an alternative holiday, the employer may designate a date.

2. The employer must give at least 14 days' notice.

3. The designated day must be reasonable having regard to the employee's circumstances.

SECTION: 53

HEADING: Alternative holiday on termination

1. If employment ends before an alternative holiday is taken, the employer must pay the employee for the outstanding alternative holiday.

2. The payment must be included in the employee's final holiday payment.

SECTION: 54

HEADING: Exchange of alternative holiday for payment

1. An employee may request payment instead of taking an alternative holiday.

2. The employer may agree to the request.

3. Payment must be made as soon as practicable after agreement.

SECTION: 55

HEADING: Expiry of alternative holiday

1. An alternative holiday does not expire merely because the employee does not take it within a specified period.

2. An employer must ensure that the employee is provided with a reasonable opportunity to take the alternative holiday.

PARTNUMBER: 5

PARTTITLE: Sick leave

SECTION: 56

HEADING: Entitlement to sick leave

1. An employee is entitled to 10 days' paid sick leave for each 12-month period of employment after satisfying the eligibility requirements.

2. An employer may provide additional sick leave.

SECTION: 57

HEADING: Eligibility for sick leave

An employee becomes entitled to sick leave after—

(a) completing 6 months' continuous employment; or

(b) satisfying an alternative eligibility test prescribed by regulation.

SECTION: 58

HEADING: Uses of sick leave

An employee may take sick leave if—

(a) the employee is sick or injured;

(b) the employee's spouse or partner is sick or injured;

(c) the employee's child is sick or injured; or

(d) a person who depends on the employee for care is sick or injured.

SECTION: 59

HEADING: Sick leave entitlement period

1. Sick leave is provided for each 12-month period following the employee's initial eligibility.

2. Sick leave entitlements must be recorded by the employer.

SECTION: 60

HEADING: Carrying over sick leave

1. An employee may carry unused sick leave into a subsequent entitlement period.

2. An employee may accumulate up to 20 days of sick leave unless the employment agreement provides for a greater entitlement.

SECTION: 61

HEADING: Sick leave in advance

1. An employer may agree to provide sick leave before the employee becomes entitled to it.

2. Sick leave taken in advance may be deducted from the employee's future entitlement.

SECTION: 62

HEADING: Notice of sick leave

1. An employee who intends to take sick leave must notify the employer as early as reasonably practicable.

2. Notice may be given by the employee or by another person acting on the employee's behalf.

SECTION: 63

HEADING: Proof of sickness or injury

1. An employer may require reasonable evidence of sickness or injury.

2. An employer must meet the reasonable cost of obtaining evidence where the employer requires evidence without reasonable grounds for suspicion.

SECTION: 64

HEADING: Sick leave for appointments

An employee may use sick leave for a medical, dental, counselling, or other health appointment where the employee is unable to reasonably attend outside working hours.

SECTION: 65

HEADING: Part-day sick leave

1. An employee may take sick leave for part of a working day.

2. The employee's sick leave balance must be reduced only by the amount of leave actually taken.

SECTION: 66

HEADING: Payment for sick leave

1. An employer must pay an employee for sick leave taken on an otherwise working day.

2. Payment must be calculated under Part 9.

SECTION: 67

HEADING: Sick leave during annual holidays

If an employee becomes sick during annual holidays, the employee may have the affected period treated as sick leave in accordance with [[section:26]].

SECTION: 68

HEADING: Sick leave and public holidays

A public holiday must not be deducted from an employee's sick leave balance merely because the employee is sick on that day.

PARTNUMBER: 6

PARTTITLE: Bereavement leave

SECTION: 69

HEADING: Entitlement to bereavement leave

1. An eligible employee is entitled to paid bereavement leave when the employee suffers a bereavement.

2. Bereavement leave is separate from annual holidays and sick leave.

SECTION: 70

HEADING: Close family bereavement

An employee suffers a qualifying bereavement on the death of the employee's—

(a) spouse or partner;

(b) parent;

(c) child;

(d) brother or sister;

(e) grandparent;

(f) grandchild; or

(g) spouse's or partner's parent.

SECTION: 71

HEADING: Other bereavement

1. An employee may take bereavement leave following the death of another person if the employer accepts that the employee has suffered a bereavement.

2. The employer must consider—

(a) the closeness of the relationship;

(b) the employee's responsibility for funeral arrangements;

(c) cultural responsibilities;

(d) the employee's involvement in the deceased person's care; and

(e) any other relevant circumstance.

SECTION: 72

HEADING: Duration of bereavement leave

1. An employee is entitled to at least 3 days' bereavement leave for a qualifying close-family bereavement.

2. An employee is entitled to at least 1 day for another qualifying bereavement.

3. An employer may provide additional bereavement leave.

SECTION: 73

HEADING: Multiple bereavements

If more than one qualifying bereavement occurs at substantially the same time, the employee may take the applicable entitlement for each bereavement.

SECTION: 74

HEADING: Notice of bereavement leave

An employee must notify the employer of an intention to take bereavement leave as soon as reasonably practicable.

SECTION: 75

HEADING: Evidence of bereavement

1. An employer may request reasonable evidence of a bereavement.

2. The employer must consider the cultural and personal circumstances of the employee when requesting evidence.

SECTION: 76

HEADING: Payment for bereavement leave

An employee must be paid for bereavement leave taken on an otherwise working day.

SECTION: 77

HEADING: Bereavement during annual holidays

If a qualifying bereavement occurs during annual holidays, the affected days must be treated as bereavement leave.

SECTION: 78

HEADING: Bereavement and public holidays

A public holiday occurring during a period of bereavement leave must remain a public holiday.

PARTNUMBER: 7

PARTTITLE: Family violence leave

SECTION: 79

HEADING: Entitlement to family violence leave

1. An employee affected by family violence is entitled to paid family violence leave in accordance with this Part.

2. The minimum entitlement is 10 days in each 12-month period.

SECTION: 80

HEADING: Meaning of affected by family violence

An employee is affected by family violence if—

(a) the employee is a victim of family violence;

(b) a child living with the employee is affected by family violence; or

(c) another circumstance prescribed by the Family Violence Act applies.

SECTION: 81

HEADING: Uses of family violence leave

Family violence leave may be taken for the purpose of—

(a) obtaining medical treatment;

(b) obtaining legal assistance;

(c) attending court;

(d) obtaining counselling;

(e) arranging alternative accommodation;

(f) protecting the employee or a child from family violence; or

(g) any other purpose reasonably connected with the effects of family violence.

SECTION: 82

HEADING: Notice

1. An employee must notify the employer as soon as reasonably practicable.

2. An employer must not require an employee to disclose unnecessary details concerning family violence.

SECTION: 83

HEADING: Evidence

1. An employer may request reasonable evidence of eligibility for family violence leave.

2. Evidence may include—

(a) a protection order;

(b) a report from a medical professional;

(c) a report from a social service provider;

(d) a court document; or

(e) another form of evidence reasonably sufficient to establish eligibility.

SECTION: 84

HEADING: Confidentiality

1. An employer must keep information concerning an employee's family violence leave confidential.

2. Information may be disclosed only—

(a) with the employee's consent;

(b) where required by law; or

(c) where necessary to protect a person from serious harm.

SECTION: 85

HEADING: Payment

An employee taking family violence leave must be paid in accordance with Part 9.

SECTION: 86

HEADING: Family violence leave and annual holidays

Family violence leave taken during annual holidays must not be deducted from the employee's annual holiday entitlement.

SECTION: 87

HEADING: Family violence leave and public holidays

A public holiday occurring during family violence leave remains a public holiday and must not be deducted from the employee's family violence leave balance.

PARTNUMBER: 8

PARTTITLE: Parental and related leave

SECTION: 88

HEADING: Relationship with parental leave

1. Parental leave is governed principally by the Parental Leave and Employment Protection Act.

2. Nothing in this Act reduces an entitlement provided under that Act.

SECTION: 89

HEADING: Annual holidays following parental leave

1. An employee returning from parental leave retains annual holiday entitlements that arose before the leave.

2. The employer must update the employee's holiday records to reflect the employee's return.

SECTION: 90

HEADING: Sick leave following parental leave

An employee's accrued sick leave must not be cancelled because the employee takes parental leave.

SECTION: 91

HEADING: Bereavement following parental leave

An employee returning from parental leave is entitled to bereavement leave in accordance with Part 6.

SECTION: 92

HEADING: Family violence leave following parental leave

An employee returning from parental leave is entitled to family violence leave in accordance with Part 7.

SECTION: 93

HEADING: Protection of leave entitlements

An employer must not terminate or disadvantage an employee because the employee takes parental leave or another form of leave protected by law.

PARTNUMBER: 9

PARTTITLE: Payment for holidays and leave

SECTION: 94

HEADING: General payment principle

An employee must receive at least the amount required by this Act for every paid holiday or leave day.

SECTION: 95

HEADING: Relevant daily pay

Relevant daily pay is the amount an employee would have received had the employee worked the relevant day.

SECTION: 96

HEADING: Components of relevant daily pay

Relevant daily pay may include—

(a) ordinary wages;

(b) regular allowances;

(c) regular overtime;

(d) regular commission;

(e) regular productivity payments; and

(f) other payments that would have been received for work on the relevant day.

SECTION: 97

HEADING: Average daily pay

1. Average daily pay may be used where relevant daily pay cannot reasonably be determined.

2. Average daily pay must be calculated using the employee's gross earnings over the prescribed calculation period.

SECTION: 98

HEADING: Ordinary weekly pay

Ordinary weekly pay is the amount the employee ordinarily receives for an ordinary week of work.

SECTION: 99

HEADING: Average weekly earnings

Average weekly earnings must be calculated by dividing the employee's gross earnings during the relevant 12-month period by the number of weeks in that period.

SECTION: 100

HEADING: Annual holiday payment

An employee taking annual holidays must receive the greater of—

(a) the employee's ordinary weekly pay attributable to the holiday period; or

(b) the employee's average weekly earnings attributable to the holiday period.

SECTION: 101

HEADING: Public holiday payment

An employee who does not work on a public holiday that is an otherwise working day must receive the employee's relevant daily pay or average daily pay.

SECTION: 102

HEADING: Alternative holiday payment

An alternative holiday must be paid at the employee's relevant daily pay or average daily pay.

SECTION: 103

HEADING: Sick leave payment

Sick leave must be paid at the employee's relevant daily pay or average daily pay.

SECTION: 104

HEADING: Bereavement leave payment

Bereavement leave must be paid at the employee's relevant daily pay or average daily pay.

SECTION: 105

HEADING: Family violence leave payment

Family violence leave must be paid at the employee's relevant daily pay or average daily pay.

SECTION: 106

HEADING: Payment where hours vary

1. Where an employee's hours vary significantly, the employer must use a reasonable calculation method that reflects the employee's actual earnings.

2. The method must be recorded in the employer's records.

SECTION: 107

HEADING: Payment of allowances

Regular allowances must be included in holiday and leave calculations where the employee would otherwise have received the allowance.

SECTION: 108

HEADING: Commission payments

Regular commission payments must be included in calculations where required by this Act.

SECTION: 109

HEADING: Overtime

Regular overtime may be included in holiday and leave calculations if the employee would otherwise have worked the overtime.

SECTION: 110

HEADING: Deductions

An employer must not make a deduction from holiday or leave pay except—

(a) where authorised by law;

(b) where authorised in writing by the employee; or

(c) where otherwise permitted by the employment agreement.

PARTNUMBER: 10

PARTTITLE: Employment records

SECTION: 111

HEADING: Employer must keep records

Every employer must maintain accurate records showing each employee's—

(a) name;

(b) employment commencement date;

(c) hours worked;

(d) annual holiday entitlement;

(e) annual holidays taken;

(f) public holidays worked;

(g) alternative holidays;

(h) sick leave;

(i) bereavement leave;

(j) family violence leave;

(k) holiday payments;

(l) leave payments; and

(m) other information prescribed by regulation.

SECTION: 112

HEADING: Records of hours worked

1. An employer must keep records of the hours worked by an employee where necessary to determine holiday or leave entitlements.

2. Records may be kept electronically.

SECTION: 113

HEADING: Records of public holidays

An employer must record—

(a) every public holiday on which the employee worked;

(b) whether the day was an otherwise working day;

(c) the hours worked; and

(d) any alternative holiday arising from the work.

SECTION: 114

HEADING: Records of leave

An employer must record every period of leave taken by an employee and the balance remaining after the leave is taken.

SECTION: 115

HEADING: Employee access to records

1. An employee may request access to their holiday and leave records.

2. The employer must provide the records within 10 working days.

SECTION: 116

HEADING: Correction of records

1. An employee may request correction of an inaccurate record.

2. The employer must investigate the request.

3. If the employer refuses to correct the record, the employer must provide written reasons.

SECTION: 117

HEADING: Retention of records

1. Records required by this Act must be retained for at least 7 years.

2. A longer period may be prescribed by regulation.

SECTION: 118

HEADING: Electronic records

Electronic records must be—

(a) accessible;

(b) capable of being reproduced in a readable form;

(c) protected from unauthorised alteration; and

(d) backed up in accordance with reasonable business practices.

PARTNUMBER: 11

PARTTITLE: Annual closedowns

SECTION: 119

HEADING: Annual closedown

1. An employer may have an annual closedown period during which the employer's business or part of the business does not operate.

2. The employer may require employees to take annual holidays during the closedown period.

SECTION: 120

HEADING: Notice of closedown

An employer must give employees at least 14 days' notice of an annual closedown.

SECTION: 121

HEADING: Closedown period

1. An annual closedown period must not exceed 2 weeks unless otherwise agreed.

2. An employer may establish more than one closedown period where different parts of the business operate at different times.

SECTION: 122

HEADING: Public holidays during closedown

A public holiday occurring during an annual closedown must remain a public holiday.

SECTION: 123

HEADING: Employee without sufficient annual holidays

1. If an employee does not have sufficient annual holidays to cover the closedown period, the employer must provide the employee with—

(a) annual holidays to the extent available; and

(b) unpaid leave for the remainder; or

(c) another arrangement agreed with the employee.

SECTION: 124

HEADING: New employees and closedowns

An employer may require an employee who has not yet become entitled to annual holidays to take leave during a closedown period in accordance with regulations.

SECTION: 125

HEADING: Closedown records

An employer must record the dates of every annual closedown period.

PARTNUMBER: 12

PARTTITLE: Flexible and irregular work arrangements

SECTION: 126

HEADING: Irregular work patterns

1. This Act applies to employees regardless of whether their hours are regular or irregular.

2. An employer must use a reasonable method for determining otherwise working days.

SECTION: 127

HEADING: Part-time employees

A part-time employee has the same minimum rights under this Act as a full-time employee, subject to the employee's hours and working pattern.

SECTION: 128

HEADING: Casual employees

1. A casual employee is entitled to holiday and leave entitlements where the employee satisfies the applicable eligibility requirements.

2. An employer must not classify an employee as casual for the purpose of avoiding holiday or leave obligations.

SECTION: 129

HEADING: Employees with changing hours

1. Where an employee's hours change during the year, the employer must review the employee's holiday and leave calculations.

2. The review must be conducted at least annually.

SECTION: 130

HEADING: Shift workers

1. A shift worker is entitled to the same minimum holiday and leave rights as other employees.

2. Special calculation rules may be prescribed for shift workers.

SECTION: 131

HEADING: Employees working multiple locations

An employee working at multiple locations for the same employer remains entitled to the same holiday and leave entitlements.

SECTION: 132

HEADING: Multiple employment agreements

1. Where an employee works under more than one employment agreement with the same employer, the employer must not use separate agreements to unlawfully avoid holiday or leave entitlements.

2. The Commission may treat substantially connected employment as a single employment relationship for the purposes of this Act.

PARTNUMBER: 13

PARTTITLE: Special employment circumstances

SECTION: 133

HEADING: Transfer of business

1. Where employment is transferred as part of a transfer of business, holiday and leave entitlements must be dealt with in accordance with the Employment Relations Act.

2. An employee must not lose an entitlement solely because of a transfer of employment where continuity is preserved by law.

SECTION: 134

HEADING: Sale of business

The sale of a business does not, by itself, extinguish an employee's accrued holiday or leave entitlement.

SECTION: 135

HEADING: Temporary suspension

A temporary suspension of work does not automatically terminate accrued holiday or leave entitlements.

SECTION: 136

HEADING: Strike or lockout

1. A period during which an employee is lawfully participating in a strike may affect holiday calculations only as provided by regulation.

2. An employer must not cancel an existing holiday entitlement merely because an employee has participated in lawful industrial action.

SECTION: 137

HEADING: Suspension of employee

An employee suspended on full pay continues to accrue entitlements unless otherwise provided by law.

SECTION: 138

HEADING: Employee detained or imprisoned

An employee who is absent because of detention or imprisonment is subject to the rules prescribed by employment law concerning accrual of leave.

PARTNUMBER: 14

PARTTITLE: Calculations and determination of working days

SECTION: 139

HEADING: Purpose of this Part

This Part provides rules for determining otherwise working days and calculating holiday and leave payments.

SECTION: 140

HEADING: Relevant daily pay

1. Relevant daily pay is the amount an employee would have received had the employee worked the relevant day.

2. Relevant daily pay must include payments that would otherwise have been received for work performed on that day.

SECTION: 141

HEADING: Average daily pay

1. Average daily pay is calculated by dividing the employee's gross earnings for the relevant period by the number of days in that period.

2. The Commission may prescribe alternative calculation methods where the ordinary method would produce an unreasonable result.

SECTION: 142

HEADING: Ordinary weekly pay

Ordinary weekly pay must include—

(a) ordinary wages;

(b) regular allowances;

(c) regular productivity payments; and

(d) any other payment forming part of the employee's normal weekly remuneration.

SECTION: 143

HEADING: Average weekly earnings

Average weekly earnings must be calculated using the employee's gross earnings over the 12 months immediately preceding the calculation date.

SECTION: 144

HEADING: Gross earnings

Gross earnings include all payments received by an employee for work except payments expressly excluded by regulation.

SECTION: 145

HEADING: Alternative calculation methods

The Commission may prescribe alternative methods where—

(a) the employee's hours vary;

(b) the employee has recently changed roles;

(c) the employee receives commission;

(d) the employee works irregular shifts; or

(e) another calculation would produce an unreasonable result.

SECTION: 146

HEADING: Determining an otherwise working day

1. In determining whether a day would otherwise be a working day, regard must be had to—

(a) the employee's employment agreement;

(b) the employee's work patterns;

(c) rosters;

(d) previous work;

(e) employer expectations; and

(f) any other relevant circumstances.

2. An employer must not manipulate rosters for the purpose of avoiding a holiday entitlement.

SECTION: 146A

HEADING: Presumption of otherwise working day

1. If an employee has regularly worked on a particular day during the preceding 4 weeks, the day is presumed to be an otherwise working day unless evidence indicates otherwise.

2. The presumption may be rebutted by evidence of a genuine change in the employee's work pattern.

SECTION: 146B

HEADING: Irregular schedules

Where an employee works irregularly, the employer must use a reasonable assessment of the employee's established work pattern.

SECTION: 146C

HEADING: Roster changes

1. An employer must not change a roster solely to prevent an employee obtaining an entitlement under this Act.

2. A roster change made for that purpose is ineffective.

SECTION: 146D

HEADING: Disagreement about working day

1. If an employer and employee disagree about whether a day is an otherwise working day, either party may refer the matter to the Commission.

2. The Commission may determine the matter.

PARTNUMBER: 15

PARTTITLE: Interaction between holidays and leave

SECTION: 147

HEADING: General rule

1. Each type of leave provided by this Act is separate unless this Act expressly provides otherwise.

2. An employee must not be required to use one form of leave merely because another entitlement has been exhausted.

SECTION: 148

HEADING: Annual holidays and sick leave

An employee may use sick leave instead of annual holidays where the requirements of [[section:26]] are satisfied.

SECTION: 149

HEADING: Annual holidays and bereavement leave

An employee may use bereavement leave instead of annual holidays where the requirements of [[section:27]] are satisfied.

SECTION: 150

HEADING: Annual holidays and family violence leave

An employee may use family violence leave instead of annual holidays where the requirements of [[section:28]] are satisfied.

SECTION: 151

HEADING: Sick leave and bereavement leave

1. Sick leave and bereavement leave are separate entitlements.

2. An employee may not be required to use sick leave for a qualifying bereavement.

SECTION: 152

HEADING: Sick leave and family violence leave

An employee may not be required to use sick leave for circumstances giving rise to family violence leave.

SECTION: 153

HEADING: Bereavement leave and family violence leave

Bereavement leave and family violence leave are separate entitlements.

SECTION: 154

HEADING: Public holidays during leave

A public holiday must be treated as a public holiday rather than another form of leave where the requirements of this Act are satisfied.

SECTION: 155

HEADING: Alternative holidays during leave

An alternative holiday remains available while an employee is taking another form of leave unless the employee agrees to take it during that period and the law permits it.

PARTNUMBER: 16

PARTTITLE: Requests, approvals, and administration

SECTION: 156

HEADING: Leave requests

1. An employee may request leave orally or in writing.

2. An employer may establish reasonable procedures for requesting leave.

3. Procedures must not make it unreasonably difficult for an employee to exercise an entitlement.

SECTION: 157

HEADING: Employer response

1. An employer must respond to a request for leave within a reasonable time.

2. If an employer declines a request for discretionary leave, the employer must provide reasons on request.

SECTION: 158

HEADING: Emergency leave

1. An employee may take emergency leave where circumstances arise unexpectedly and prevent the employee from attending work.

2. Emergency leave must be treated as the appropriate statutory leave where the requirements for that leave are satisfied.

SECTION: 159

HEADING: Evidence requests

1. An employer may request evidence where authorised by this Act.

2. An employer must not use evidence requirements to unreasonably delay or prevent leave.

SECTION: 160

HEADING: Confidentiality of leave information

1. Information about an employee's medical condition, bereavement, family violence, or other sensitive personal circumstances must be kept confidential.

2. An employer must limit access to such information to persons who reasonably require it.

SECTION: 161

HEADING: Privacy

An employer must handle personal information obtained under this Act in accordance with applicable privacy legislation.

PARTNUMBER: 17

PARTTITLE: Enforcement

SECTION: 162

HEADING: Enforcement by employee

1. An employee may enforce an entitlement under this Act through the Employment Relations Commission.

2. An employee may also seek relief through the Employment Court.

SECTION: 163

HEADING: Representative enforcement

A union or other authorised representative may bring proceedings on behalf of an employee with the employee's consent.

SECTION: 164

HEADING: Compliance orders

The Commission may order an employer to—

(a) provide an unpaid entitlement;

(b) correct employment records;

(c) pay outstanding holiday or leave payments;

(d) cease unlawful conduct; or

(e) take another action necessary to comply with this Act.

SECTION: 165

HEADING: Recovery of unpaid entitlements

1. An employee may recover unpaid holiday or leave payments as a debt.

2. The Commission may order interest on unpaid amounts.

SECTION: 166

HEADING: Employer audits

1. The Commission may audit an employer's compliance with this Act.

2. An employer must provide records reasonably required for the audit.

SECTION: 167

HEADING: Inspection powers

An authorised officer may inspect employment records for the purpose of determining compliance with this Act.

SECTION: 168

HEADING: Information notices

1. The Commission may require an employer to provide information relevant to compliance.

2. An employer must comply within the period specified in the notice.

SECTION: 169

HEADING: Failure to comply

A person who knowingly fails to comply with a lawful requirement of the Commission commits an offence.

PARTNUMBER: 18

PARTTITLE: Offences

SECTION: 170

HEADING: Failure to provide minimum entitlement

An employer commits an offence if the employer knowingly fails to provide a minimum entitlement under this Act.

SECTION: 171

HEADING: False records

An employer commits an offence if the employer knowingly creates, maintains, or provides a materially false holiday or leave record.

SECTION: 172

HEADING: Destruction of records

A person commits an offence if the person knowingly destroys, conceals, or alters a record required to be retained under this Act for the purpose of avoiding compliance.

SECTION: 173

HEADING: Obstruction

A person commits an offence if the person intentionally obstructs an authorised officer exercising a power under this Act.

SECTION: 174

HEADING: Retaliation against employee

An employer commits an offence if the employer knowingly penalises an employee for exercising a right under this Act.

SECTION: 175

HEADING: Coercion to cash up holidays

An employer commits an offence if the employer coerces or improperly pressures an employee to exchange annual holidays for payment.

SECTION: 176

HEADING: Maximum penalties

1. An individual convicted of an offence under this Part is liable to a fine not exceeding ₴50,000.

2. A body corporate convicted of an offence under this Part is liable to a fine not exceeding ₴250,000.

3. The court may order an employer to pay outstanding entitlements in addition to any penalty.

PARTNUMBER: 19

PARTTITLE: Disputes

SECTION: 177

HEADING: Dispute about entitlement

A dispute concerning an entitlement under this Act may be referred to the Employment Relations Commission.

SECTION: 178

HEADING: Mediation

1. The Commission may direct the parties to participate in mediation.

2. Mediation proceedings are confidential unless the parties agree otherwise.

SECTION: 179

HEADING: Determination

1. If a dispute cannot be resolved through agreement, the Commission may determine the dispute.

2. A determination may include—

(a) payment of outstanding entitlements;

(b) correction of records;

(c) compensation;

(d) penalties; and

(e) any other remedy authorised by law.

SECTION: 180

HEADING: Appeal

A party dissatisfied with a determination may appeal to the Employment Court in accordance with the Employment Relations Act.

SECTION: 181

HEADING: Limitation period

1. A claim for an unpaid entitlement must generally be brought within 6 years after the entitlement became payable.

2. The Commission may extend the period where exceptional circumstances justify doing so.

PARTNUMBER: 20

PARTTITLE: Powers of the Employment Relations Commission

SECTION: 182

HEADING: General powers

The Commission may—

(a) investigate alleged breaches;

(b) require information;

(c) inspect records;

(d) issue compliance orders;

(e) facilitate mediation;

(f) make determinations; and

(g) perform any other function conferred by this Act.

SECTION: 183

HEADING: Guidelines

1. The Commission may issue guidelines concerning compliance with this Act.

2. Guidelines must be published.

3. Guidelines do not have the force of law unless authorised by legislation.

SECTION: 184

HEADING: Compliance notices

1. The Commission may issue a compliance notice where it reasonably believes that an employer is failing to comply.

2. A compliance notice may specify—

(a) the contravention;

(b) the steps required to remedy it; and

(c) the deadline for compliance.

SECTION: 185

HEADING: Review of compliance notice

An employer may apply to the Commission for review of a compliance notice within 14 days after receiving it.

SECTION: 186

HEADING: Failure to comply with notice

Failure to comply with a compliance notice without reasonable excuse is an offence.

PARTNUMBER: 21

PARTTITLE: Special rules for employers

SECTION: 187

HEADING: Employer responsibility

1. An employer is responsible for ensuring that employees receive their minimum entitlements.

2. An employer must not rely solely on an employee to identify errors in holiday or leave calculations.

SECTION: 188

HEADING: Payroll systems

1. An employer must maintain a payroll system capable of accurately recording statutory holiday and leave entitlements.

2. An employer with more than 20 employees must conduct an annual review of its payroll compliance.

SECTION: 189

HEADING: Payroll errors

1. If an employer discovers an error in holiday or leave payments, the employer must correct the error as soon as reasonably practicable.

2. The correction must include any amount that should have been paid to the employee.

SECTION: 190

HEADING: Systemic errors

1. If an employer discovers that a payroll error affects multiple employees, the employer must investigate the extent of the error.

2. The employer must correct all affected records.

3. The employer must notify the Commission if the error materially affects 20 or more employees.

SECTION: 191

HEADING: Employer insolvency

Where an employer becomes insolvent, unpaid holiday and leave entitlements rank as employment-related debts to the extent provided by insolvency law.

PARTNUMBER: 22

PARTTITLE: Special rules for employees

SECTION: 192

HEADING: Employee responsibility

An employee must provide reasonable information necessary to determine an entitlement under this Act.

SECTION: 193

HEADING: Employee not required to calculate entitlement

An employee is not required to calculate their own statutory entitlement as a condition of receiving it.

SECTION: 194

HEADING: Protection of employee

An employee must not be disadvantaged for—

(a) taking statutory leave;

(b) requesting statutory leave;

(c) making a complaint;

(d) participating in an investigation; or

(e) enforcing an entitlement.

SECTION: 195

HEADING: Change of employment

A change from full-time to part-time employment does not extinguish accrued entitlements.

SECTION: 196

HEADING: Return to employment

Where an employee returns to employment with the same employer after an approved period of leave, accrued entitlements must be restored in accordance with this Act.

PARTNUMBER: 23

PARTTITLE: Public holiday administration

SECTION: 197

HEADING: Additional public holidays

The Governor-General may, by Order in Council, declare an additional day to be a public holiday for a specified year.

SECTION: 198

HEADING: National occasions

Parliament may declare a day to be a public holiday to commemorate a significant national event.

SECTION: 199

HEADING: Local public holidays

1. A public holiday may be declared for a particular region or locality.

2. A local public holiday applies only to employees whose employment is principally based in the relevant area.

SECTION: 200

HEADING: Substitute public holidays

1. The Minister may prescribe substitute days where necessary to accommodate—

(a) national events;

(b) calendar arrangements;

(c) exceptional circumstances; or

(d) another reason of significant public importance.

2. A substitute day has the same legal effect as the public holiday for which it is substituted.

SECTION: 201

HEADING: Public holiday calendar

The Minister must publish an official public holiday calendar before the beginning of each calendar year.

PARTNUMBER: 24

PARTTITLE: Regulations

SECTION: 202

HEADING: Regulations

The Governor-General may, by Order in Council, make regulations for the purposes of this Act.

SECTION: 203

HEADING: Matters that may be prescribed

Regulations may prescribe—

(a) methods for calculating holiday and leave payments;

(b) record-keeping requirements;

(c) forms;

(d) evidence requirements;

(e) procedures for inspections;

(f) procedures for disputes;

(g) rules concerning irregular employees;

(h) rules concerning shift workers;

(i) rules concerning annual closedowns;

(j) public holiday arrangements;

(k) electronic record requirements; and

(l) any other matter necessary to give effect to this Act.

SECTION: 204

HEADING: Regulations concerning calculations

Regulations may prescribe alternative calculation methods where standard calculations would produce an unreasonable or impracticable result.

SECTION: 205

HEADING: Regulations concerning evidence

Regulations may prescribe—

(a) acceptable forms of evidence;

(b) time limits for providing evidence;

(c) procedures for verifying evidence; and

(d) circumstances in which evidence may not be required.

PARTNUMBER: 25

PARTTITLE: Review and reporting

SECTION: 206

HEADING: Annual report on holidays

The Minister must include in the Ministry's annual report information concerning—

(a) compliance with this Act;

(b) enforcement action;

(c) complaints;

(d) unpaid holiday entitlements;

(e) significant employment trends affecting leave; and

(f) recommendations for legislative reform.

SECTION: 207

HEADING: Five-year review

1. The Minister must arrange a review of this Act at least once every 5 years.

2. The review must consider—

(a) whether minimum entitlements remain adequate;

(b) whether the Act is understandable;

(c) whether employers can reasonably comply;

(d) whether employees are receiving their entitlements;

(e) whether payment calculations remain appropriate;

(f) whether the public holiday system remains appropriate; and

(g) whether amendments are necessary.

SECTION: 208

HEADING: Parliamentary report

A report prepared under [[section:207]] must be presented to Parliament within 20 sitting days after completion.

PARTNUMBER: 26

PARTTITLE: Transitional provisions

SECTION: 209

HEADING: Existing entitlements

1. An entitlement accrued under the Holidays Act or another enactment before the commencement of this Act remains enforceable.

2. An employer must not retrospectively remove an accrued entitlement.

SECTION: 210

HEADING: Existing employment agreements

1. An employment agreement in force immediately before commencement continues in force.

2. Any provision inconsistent with this Act is modified to the extent necessary to comply with this Act.

SECTION: 211

HEADING: Existing holiday balances

1. Annual holiday balances existing immediately before commencement must be transferred to the employee's holiday record under this Act.

2. Sick leave balances must also be transferred.

3. Alternative holiday balances must be preserved.

SECTION: 212

HEADING: Transitional calculations

1. The Minister may prescribe transitional calculation methods for employees whose entitlement periods overlap the commencement of this Act.

2. A transitional method must not reduce an employee's accrued entitlement.

SECTION: 213

HEADING: Existing disputes

A proceeding concerning an entitlement under an earlier enactment that was commenced before the commencement of this Act may continue under the earlier enactment unless the parties agree otherwise.

SECTION: 214

HEADING: References to repealed legislation

A reference in an employment agreement to the Holidays Act 2003 or another repealed holiday enactment must, so far as practicable, be read as a reference to the corresponding provision of this Act.

PARTNUMBER: 27

PARTTITLE: Miscellaneous provisions

SECTION: 215

HEADING: Crown employees

1. Employees of the Crown are entitled to the minimum entitlements under this Act.

2. The Crown must comply with this Act in the same manner as any other employer.

SECTION: 216

HEADING: Local authorities

Local authorities are employers for the purposes of this Act.

SECTION: 217

HEADING: Public corporations

A public corporation is an employer for the purposes of this Act.

SECTION: 218

HEADING: Contractors

1. This Act does not apply to a person who is genuinely an independent contractor.

2. A person must not be classified as an independent contractor for the purpose of avoiding an entitlement under this Act.

SECTION: 219

HEADING: Employee status

The Employment Relations Commission or Employment Court may determine whether a person is an employee for the purposes of this Act.

SECTION: 220

HEADING: Severability

If a provision of this Act is held to be invalid, the remaining provisions continue in force to the extent that they can operate independently.

SECTION: 221

HEADING: No contracting out

1. An employer and employee must not contract out of this Act.

2. Subsection (1) does not prevent an employer from providing an entitlement more favourable than the entitlement required by this Act.

SECTION: 222

HEADING: Greater contractual entitlements

1. An employment agreement may provide for—

(a) additional annual holidays;

(b) additional sick leave;

(c) additional bereavement leave;

(d) additional family violence leave;

(e) higher public holiday rates;

(f) additional alternative holidays; or

(g) any other more favourable entitlement.

2. A greater contractual entitlement must not be used to disguise a reduction in another statutory entitlement.

SECTION: 223

HEADING: Employer policies

1. An employer may establish policies concerning holidays and leave.

2. A policy must comply with this Act.

3. An employee must not be disadvantaged because a policy provides less than the minimum entitlement under this Act.

PARTNUMBER: 28

PARTTITLE: Final provisions

SECTION: 224

HEADING: Repeal

The Holidays Act 2003 is repealed.

SECTION: 225

HEADING: Consequential amendments

The enactments specified in Schedule 1 are amended as set out in that Schedule.

SECTION: 226

HEADING: Savings

1. Anything lawfully done under an enactment repealed by this Act remains valid.

2. Any notice, determination, agreement, record, or proceeding made under a repealed enactment continues to have effect to the extent that it is consistent with this Act.

SECTION: 227

HEADING: Crown liability

Nothing in this Act limits any right of an employee to bring proceedings against the Crown where the Crown has failed to comply with this Act.

SECTION: 228

HEADING: Act to be administered by Minister

This Act is administered by the Minister responsible for employment.

SECTION: 229

HEADING: Review of administration

The Minister must monitor the operation of this Act and may recommend amendments to Parliament where necessary.

SECTION: 230

HEADING: Commencement of repeal

The repeal of the Holidays Act 2003 takes effect on the commencement of this Act.

SECTION: 231

HEADING: Expiry of transitional powers

Any power to make transitional regulations under this Act expires 3 years after the commencement of this Act unless extended by Act of Parliament.

SECTION: 232

HEADING: Act prevails over inconsistent employment agreement

If an employment agreement is inconsistent with this Act, this Act prevails to the extent of the inconsistency.

SECTION: 233

HEADING: Act does not limit better conditions

Nothing in this Act prevents an employer from providing an employee with conditions more favourable than those required by this Act.

SECTION: 234

HEADING: Protection of accrued rights

An employee's accrued holiday, leave, or payment entitlement must not be extinguished solely because this Act replaces another enactment.

SECTION: 235

HEADING: Final interpretation

This Act must be interpreted in a manner consistent with its purpose of providing employees with fair, predictable, and enforceable minimum entitlements to holidays and leave.

SCHEDULENUMBER: 1

SCHEDULETITLE: Consequential Amendments

SCHEDULESECTION: 1

HEADING: Amendments

(1) The Employment Relations Act is amended by inserting references to the Employment Holidays Act 2026 wherever necessary.
(2) The Employment Relations Act is amended to provide that disputes concerning holiday and leave entitlements may be referred to the Employment Relations Commission.
(3) The Electoral and other public administration legislation is amended to recognise the public holidays established under this Act.
(4) Any reference to the Holidays Act 2003 is replaced by a reference to the Employment Holidays Act 2026.

SCHEDULESECTION: 2

HEADING: Repeals

(1) The Holidays Act 2003 is repealed.
(2) Any regulations made under the Holidays Act 2003 remain in force to the extent that they are not inconsistent with this Act until replaced or revoked.
(3) Any order declaring a public holiday under repealed legislation remains effective unless revoked or replaced.
