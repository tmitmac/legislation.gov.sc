TITLE: Great Northern National Park Bill

ACTNUMBER: 192—1

TYPE: Bill

STATUS: Current

VERSION: Latest

VERSIONTITLE: Introduction 18 August 2026

AGENCY: Ministry for the Environment; and Wildlife Conservation Agency

COMMENTARYTITLE: As reported from the Environment and Conservation Committee

COMMENTARYHEADING: Commentary

COMMENTARYSECTION: Recommendation

The Environment and Conservation Committee has examined the Bill and recommends by majority that it be passed, with the amendments set out in this commentary.

The Committee considers that the Bill would establish an important statutory framework for the protection, administration, and public enjoyment of the Great Northern National Park.

The Committee considers that the Bill provides a comprehensive framework for the establishment of the Park, the protection of its natural and geological features, the conservation of indigenous flora and fauna, public access and recreation, commercial activities, enforcement, emergency management, scientific research, infrastructure, funding, pest management, and the long-term administration of the Park.

The Committee recognises that the establishment of a national park of this scale has implications for conservation, recreation, tourism, infrastructure, land management, public access, and government expenditure.

The majority considers that the Bill strikes an appropriate balance between protecting the Park's conservation values and allowing the public to access, enjoy, and make responsible use of the Park.

The Committee has nevertheless recommended a number of amendments to improve the clarity of the legislation, strengthen administrative safeguards, clarify enforcement powers, and ensure that the Park's long-term conservation purpose remains central to decisions made under the Act.

COMMENTARYSECTION: About the Bill as introduced

The Great Northern National Park Bill (the **Bill**) establishes the Great Northern National Park and provides for its long-term protection, administration, and management.

The Bill would establish the Park as a national park for the purposes of the Act and would provide that the Park is to be managed in accordance with the Act and a statutory Park management plan.

The Bill's stated purposes include—

• establishing the Great Northern National Park;

• protecting, preserving, and enhancing the natural, scenic, ecological, geological, and recreational values of the Park;

• providing for responsible public enjoyment of the Park;

• establishing a statutory framework for the administration and management of the Park;

• protecting indigenous flora and fauna; and

• ensuring that the Park is managed for the benefit and enjoyment of present and future generations.

The Committee considers that these purposes provide an appropriate foundation for the legislation.

The Bill would also expressly provide that the Act binds the Crown and would establish rules governing the interaction between the Act and other enactments concerning wildlife, forests, waterways, biosecurity, public health and safety, roads, and historic or archaeological sites.

The Committee considers that these provisions are necessary because activities within the Park may be subject to several different statutory regimes.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Establishment and status of the Park

Part 2 establishes the Great Northern National Park.

The Bill provides that the Park is to be managed in accordance with the Act and the Park management plan.

The boundaries of the Park are set out in Schedule 1, with the Bill providing mechanisms for correcting clerical or surveying errors and for adding Crown land where appropriate.

The Committee considers that the ability to correct surveying errors is necessary for the effective administration of the Park, but that such a power must not be capable of being used to substantially alter the boundaries established by Parliament.

The Bill therefore provides that a correction must not substantially alter the area or character of the Park.

The Bill also provides a strong level of parliamentary protection for the Park's boundaries.

Land forming part of the Park cannot be removed from the Park except by an Act of Parliament.

The Minister must not recommend removal of land unless satisfied that there is an exceptional public interest in the removal, that there is no reasonably practicable alternative, and that the environmental consequences have been fully assessed.

The Committee considers these safeguards appropriate.

The Committee considers that the statutory protection of the Park should be stronger than an ordinary administrative designation because the creation of a national park represents a long-term commitment by Parliament to conservation and public enjoyment.

The Bill accordingly provides that the Park is of national importance for conservation, recreation, scientific research, education, and public enjoyment.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Objects and principles of management

The Bill establishes a series of objects for the management of the Park.

These include the preservation of indigenous ecosystems, protection of indigenous species and habitats, preservation of landscapes and geological features, protection of waterways, maintenance of ecological integrity, public recreation, scientific research, education, protection of historic and archaeological sites, and prevention of activities that would permanently or materially degrade the Park.

The Committee considers that these objects appropriately recognise that a national park has several legitimate functions.

The Park is not intended to be an area from which the public is excluded. Rather, the legislation seeks to provide for public recreation in a manner consistent with conservation.

The Committee considers that this distinction is important.

The Bill also establishes a precautionary approach for decisions made under the Act where there is a risk of serious or irreversible environmental damage.

The Committee supports this provision and considers that it provides an important safeguard where there may be uncertainty concerning the environmental consequences of a proposed activity.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Administration and governance

Part 3 establishes the administrative framework for the Park.

The Ministry is responsible for administering the Act and must ensure that the Park is managed consistently with its purposes and objects.

The Bill also establishes the Great Northern National Park Board.

The Board is intended to provide independent advice to the Minister and Ministry concerning the management and protection of the Park.

The Committee considers that the Board will provide an important source of specialist advice and public accountability.

The Bill provides for between 7 and 11 members of the Board.

In appointing members, the Minister must seek to ensure that the Board collectively possesses appropriate knowledge and experience in conservation, ecology, forestry, recreation, tourism, environmental science, local government, land management, and public administration.

The Committee considers that the range of expertise specified in the Bill is appropriate given the breadth of issues that will arise in the administration of the Park.

The Committee recommends, however, that appointments should also have regard to experience in emergency management, public safety, and community engagement.

The Committee therefore recommends an amendment to clarify that these areas may also be considered when determining the collective expertise of the Board.

The Bill provides for 4-year terms and permits reappointment, subject to a limit of 3 consecutive terms unless exceptional circumstances justify a further appointment.

The Committee considers that the term limits appropriately balance institutional knowledge with the need for periodic renewal.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Board accountability

The Board is required to meet at least 4 times each calendar year and must keep minutes of its meetings.

The Board must also provide the Minister with an annual report concerning the administration and condition of the Park.

The Committee considers annual reporting particularly important because the Park will involve public expenditure and the exercise of statutory powers.

The Committee recommends that the annual report should be made publicly available.

The Committee further recommends that the report should contain sufficient information to allow Parliament and the public to assess the condition of the Park and the effectiveness of its management.

The Committee recommends that the reporting provisions expressly refer to—

• significant changes in the ecological condition of the Park;

• major enforcement actions;

• significant closures;

• major commercial concessions;

• expenditure from the Great Northern National Park Fund; and

• progress against the Park management plan.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Park management plan

Part 4 establishes a statutory Park management plan.

The Committee considers the management plan to be one of the most important mechanisms in the Bill.

The management plan will provide the detailed framework through which the broad statutory purposes of the Act are applied to the practical management of the Park.

The Bill requires the plan to address conservation objectives, biodiversity, fire management, pest control, visitor management, tracks and roads, camping, hunting and fishing, scientific research, commercial activities, emergency management, waste, waterways, historic sites, and climate and environmental resilience.

The Committee considers the breadth of these requirements appropriate.

The Committee also supports the requirement for public consultation before a management plan is approved.

The Committee considers that public consultation is particularly important because management decisions may affect recreational users, businesses, researchers, conservation organisations, neighbouring landholders, and the wider public.

The Committee recommends an amendment requiring the Minister to publish a statement summarising the submissions received and explaining significant changes made to the draft management plan as a result of consultation.

The Committee considers that this would improve transparency without requiring every submission to be reproduced.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Conservation and environmental protection

Part 5 contains the principal environmental protection provisions of the Bill.

The Bill establishes a general duty requiring persons entering or undertaking activities within the Park to take reasonable steps to avoid unnecessary environmental damage.

The Bill prohibits unauthorised interference with indigenous vegetation and wildlife.

It also regulates the introduction of animals, plants, seeds, fungi, microorganisms, pests, and other organisms.

The Committee considers these provisions necessary to protect the ecological integrity of the Park.

The Bill also establishes restrictions concerning fire, firearms, pollution, waterways, mining, commercial forestry, waste, natural features, caves, and geological features.

The Committee strongly supports the prohibition on mining, quarrying, drilling, gravel extraction, and mineral extraction within the Park.

The Committee considers that the conservation purpose of the Park would be undermined if commercial resource extraction were permitted as an ordinary use of Park land.

The Bill nevertheless allows material to be removed where authorised by the Director for track construction, erosion control, public safety, or restoration.

The Committee considers these exceptions appropriate because limited removal of material may sometimes be necessary to maintain the Park or protect visitors.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Public access and recreation

Part 6 establishes a statutory right for members of the public to enter and travel through the Park for lawful recreational purposes.

The Committee considers public access to be a fundamental component of the Bill.

The establishment of a national park should provide opportunities for members of the public to experience and enjoy the natural environment.

The right of access is nevertheless subject to the Act, regulations, the management plan, reasonable directions of Park officers, and temporary closures.

The Committee considers these limitations necessary to protect the environment and public safety.

The Bill provides for walking tracks, vehicle access, cycling, camping, permanent structures, dogs and domestic animals, drones, organised events, and visitor safety.

The Committee considers that the framework appropriately recognises that different recreational activities can have different environmental effects.

The Committee recommends that decisions to restrict recreational access should, where practicable, be proportionate to the environmental or safety risk giving rise to the restriction.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Camping and visitor facilities

The Committee has considered the provisions governing camping and visitor facilities.

The Bill provides that camping may occur only at designated campsites or with a permit.

The Committee considers that designated camping areas will be important for controlling environmental impacts, managing waste, reducing fire risk, and ensuring visitor safety.

The Bill also permits the Ministry to establish visitor centres, information facilities, museums, educational facilities, toilets, huts, campsites, and other public amenities.

The Committee considers that infrastructure should be provided according to the environmental sensitivity and character of each part of the Park.

The Committee recommends that the management plan should identify areas where visitor infrastructure is appropriate and areas where infrastructure should be limited.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Permits, concessions, and commercial activities

Part 7 establishes a permit and concession system for activities undertaken within the Park.

The Committee considers that commercial activities may provide benefits to visitors and contribute to tourism and regional economic activity.

However, commercial use must remain subordinate to the conservation purposes of the Park.

The Bill therefore requires permitted activities to be consistent with the Act and management plan and not cause unacceptable environmental damage.

The Committee supports these requirements.

The Bill allows concessions for accommodation, guided tours, transport, food services, visitor facilities, educational services, and other compatible activities.

The Committee considers that concessions should not be treated as a means of transferring control of Park land to private interests.

The Committee therefore supports the provision that a concession must not confer ownership of Park land.

The Committee recommends that concession agreements of significant duration or scale should be subject to additional public disclosure requirements.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Enforcement and Park officers

Part 8 establishes the enforcement framework.

The Director may appoint Park rangers, while the Minister may appoint conservation officers for enforcement purposes.

Rangers may patrol the Park, assist visitors, enforce the Act, issue authorised directions, investigate suspected offences, protect Park property, and assist in emergencies.

The Committee considers that enforcement powers are necessary to ensure that the environmental protections in the Bill are effective in practice.

The Bill allows enforcement officers to require identifying information from persons reasonably suspected of committing an offence, direct persons to leave the Park in specified circumstances, and seize things reasonably believed to have been used in an offence or to constitute evidence.

The Committee has considered whether these powers contain adequate safeguards.

The Committee recommends amendments clarifying that enforcement powers must be exercised reasonably and proportionately and that officers should provide identification when exercising coercive powers, except where doing so would be impracticable because of an immediate emergency.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Offences and penalties

Part 9 establishes offences and penalties.

The Bill creates a general offence for knowingly or recklessly contravening the Act or a permit condition.

It also establishes a more serious offence where a person intentionally causes substantial or permanent environmental damage.

The Committee considers that a distinction between ordinary offences and serious environmental offences is appropriate.

The penalties must reflect the seriousness of the conduct.

The Bill provides for fines of up to ₴5,000 for minor offences and, for serious environmental offences, a fine of up to ₴100,000 or imprisonment for up to 2 years, or both.

Higher maximum fines apply to bodies corporate.

The Committee considers that the penalty framework should be capable of responding to serious environmental harm while remaining proportionate.

The Committee recommends that the sentencing provisions make clear that a court may consider the extent of environmental damage, whether the conduct was deliberate, the financial benefit obtained, the offender's previous history, and the cost of remediation when determining an appropriate penalty.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Restoration and environmental liability

The Committee supports the Bill's provisions allowing persons responsible for environmental damage to be required to restore affected areas, remove unlawful structures or materials, undertake rehabilitation work, or pay reasonable restoration costs.

The Committee considers that environmental enforcement should not depend solely on monetary punishment.

Where environmental damage can be repaired, restoration should be an important component of the response.

The Bill also allows courts to order forfeiture of equipment, restoration of damaged land, payment of Crown costs, and removal of unlawful structures.

The Committee recommends that restoration orders should be capable of being made in addition to financial penalties where appropriate.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Fire, emergencies, and closures

Part 10 provides powers to deal with fire, severe weather, flooding, disease, environmental damage, dangerous wildlife, search and rescue operations, and other significant threats.

The Committee considers these provisions essential to the safe operation of the Park.

National parks may contain significant risks arising from fire, extreme weather, terrain, wildlife, and other natural hazards.

The Bill therefore allows the Director to temporarily close all or part of the Park where necessary.

The Committee supports this power but considers that closures should be communicated to the public as effectively as reasonably practicable.

The Committee recommends that information about significant closures should, where practicable, be published through the Ministry's website and other appropriate public communication channels.

The Committee also supports emergency directions and evacuation powers.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Scientific research and education

Part 11 provides for scientific research and educational activities.

The Committee considers scientific research to be an important function of the Park.

Research undertaken in the Park may improve understanding of its ecosystems, species, geology, waterways, and environmental pressures.

The Bill allows research permits to authorise activities that would otherwise be prohibited where the research is justified and appropriately controlled.

The Committee considers this approach appropriate.

Research activities must nevertheless be subject to conditions designed to prevent unnecessary environmental damage.

The Bill also requires the Director to encourage educational use of the Park where consistent with conservation.

The Committee supports this provision and considers that national parks can provide important opportunities for environmental education and scientific learning.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Hunting, fishing, and natural resource use

Part 12 provides a statutory framework for hunting, fishing, and the collection of natural materials.

The Bill does not establish an unrestricted right to hunt or fish within the Park.

Instead, hunting may be prohibited in designated areas, while the Minister may designate specified areas where controlled hunting may occur.

Fishing may also be permitted in specified waterways, subject to restrictions concerning species, seasons, catch limits, methods, and locations.

The Committee considers that these activities should be managed according to the conservation objectives of the Park.

The Committee recommends that decisions concerning hunting and fishing should be consistent with the management plan and informed by available scientific information.

The Bill also permits restrictions to protect threatened or endangered species.

The Committee supports these provisions.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Heritage and archaeological protection

Part 13 protects historic structures, monuments, archaeological sites, and other places of historic significance within the Park.

The Committee considers it appropriate that the Bill protect both natural and historic values.

The Park's significance may extend beyond its ecosystems and landscapes to places and objects that provide evidence of previous human activity.

The Bill also establishes obligations where a person discovers an object or site that may have archaeological significance.

The Committee supports the requirement to cease disturbing such material and notify the Ministry.

The Committee recommends that public information concerning these obligations should be included in visitor information and educational material.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Infrastructure and development

Part 14 provides for infrastructure and development within the Park.

The Ministry may construct and maintain roads, bridges, walking tracks, huts, visitor centres, toilets, campsites, signs, communication facilities, and other infrastructure necessary for the administration and enjoyment of the Park.

The Committee considers that appropriate infrastructure is necessary for safe and responsible public access.

At the same time, infrastructure can have significant environmental effects.

The Bill therefore requires environmental assessment before major development and prohibits development where its effects would be inconsistent with the fundamental conservation purposes of the Park.

The Committee supports this approach.

The Committee recommends that environmental assessments should consider cumulative effects as well as the effects of individual developments.

The Committee notes that the Bill already expressly requires consideration of cumulative environmental effects and considers this an important safeguard.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Funding and financial administration

Part 15 establishes the Great Northern National Park Fund.

Money may be paid into the Fund from Parliamentary appropriations, fees and charges, concession payments, donations and grants accepted by the Ministry, and other authorised sources.

The Fund may be used for conservation, infrastructure, scientific research, visitor facilities, environmental restoration, ranger services, fire management, pest control, and administration.

The Committee supports the establishment of a dedicated Fund.

The Committee considers that money generated through activities within the Park should, where appropriate, contribute to the protection and management of the Park.

The Bill also provides that there is no general admission fee merely for entering the Park on foot unless authorised by an Act of Parliament.

The Committee supports this provision.

The Committee considers that access to the Park should remain generally available to the public without a general entry charge.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Pests, invasive species, and restoration

Part 16 provides for pest management and ecological restoration.

The Committee considers pest control to be an essential component of protecting the Park's ecological integrity.

The Bill allows the Director to establish programmes for the control or eradication of invasive species and pests and provides for methods including trapping, poisoning, fencing, removal, and biological control.

The Committee recognises that pest control operations may sometimes affect visitors and recreational activities.

The requirement to provide public notice where reasonably practicable is therefore supported.

The Committee recommends that public notices should identify the affected area, the general nature of the operation, and any relevant visitor safety precautions.

The Bill also provides for ecological restoration of degraded forests, wetlands, waterways, grasslands, threatened-species habitats, and other damaged ecosystems.

The Committee supports these provisions.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Regulations and bylaws

Part 17 provides for regulations and bylaws.

The Committee considers delegated legislation necessary because the management of the Park will require detailed rules capable of responding to changing environmental, recreational, and operational circumstances.

Regulations may address camping, fees, permits, concessions, hunting, fishing, vehicles, fires, dogs, drones, commercial activities, public events, waste, scientific research, conservation activities, and other matters necessary to give effect to the Act.

The Bill also permits the Minister to make bylaws concerning particular areas.

The Committee considers it important that regulations and bylaws remain within the purposes and limits established by Parliament.

The Committee therefore supports section 122, which prevents regulations and bylaws from authorising conduct inconsistent with the fundamental conservation purposes of the Park.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Review and parliamentary accountability

The Bill requires the Minister to review the operation of the Act at least once every 10 years.

The Committee considers periodic review appropriate given that environmental conditions, visitor patterns, scientific knowledge, and management practices may change over time.

The Bill also requires an annual State of the Park report to be presented to Parliament.

The Committee strongly supports this provision.

The annual report will provide Parliament with an opportunity to scrutinise the ecological condition of the Park, significant environmental threats, visitor numbers, major developments, enforcement activity, expenditure, conservation achievements, and future priorities.

The Committee recommends that the report should be tabled in Parliament and made publicly available.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Transitional provisions

Part 18 contains transitional and miscellaneous provisions.

The Bill protects lawful existing rights and interests and provides for the continuation of existing agreements and facilities subject to the new statutory framework.

The Committee considers these provisions necessary to avoid unnecessary disruption when the Park is established.

The Bill also provides that existing permits and authorisations relating to land incorporated into the Park remain valid for 12 months after commencement unless cancelled or replaced earlier.

The Committee considers the 12-month transitional period appropriate because it provides time for the Ministry and affected permit holders to review existing arrangements.

The Committee recommends that the Ministry should provide guidance to affected permit holders before the expiry of the transitional period.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Existing public roads

The Committee has specifically considered the provision concerning public roads within the Park.

The Bill provides that a public road within the boundaries of the Park remains a public road unless lawfully closed under another enactment.

The Committee considers this provision important because establishment of the Park should not, by itself, extinguish existing public access arrangements.

Any future closure of a public road should therefore continue to be dealt with under the applicable road legislation.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Protection from liability

The Bill provides protection from personal liability for persons acting in good faith in the exercise of powers or functions under the Act.

It also limits Crown liability for certain losses suffered merely because a person enters the Park, uses a track or facility, relies on information supplied by a Park officer, or is unable to undertake an activity because an area has been closed.

The Committee considers that appropriate statutory protections are necessary for officers performing public functions.

However, these protections should not prevent liability arising from unlawful conduct, bad faith, or conduct outside the lawful exercise of statutory powers.

The Committee recommends an amendment to clarify this principle.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Parliamentary protection of Park land

The Committee considers the provisions protecting the permanent status of the Park to be among the most significant provisions of the Bill.

The Bill provides that no provision of the Act authorises the permanent disposal of land forming part of the Park.

Land cannot be removed from the Park except by an Act of Parliament.

The Committee considers that these provisions provide an appropriate level of democratic protection.

The permanent alteration of the boundaries of a national park should not occur solely through administrative decision-making.

Parliament should retain control over any fundamental reduction in the area of the Park.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Enlargement of the Park

The Bill requires the Minister to review the boundaries of the Park at least once every 15 years.

The purpose of the review is to determine whether additional land should be incorporated into the Park.

The Minister must consult the Board before completing the review.

The Bill also allows the Minister to recommend to Parliament that additional land be incorporated where the land has significant ecological, scenic, geological, recreational, conservation, cultural, or historical value.

The Committee supports these provisions.

The Committee considers that the creation of the Park should not be viewed as the final determination of its boundaries for all time.

Future scientific knowledge, conservation requirements, and opportunities to protect connected ecosystems may justify enlargement.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Consequential amendments

Schedule 2 provides consequential amendments to existing legislation.

These amendments are intended to ensure that the Great Northern National Park is properly recognised within the wider statutory framework.

The Committee considers these amendments necessary to avoid uncertainty about the legal status of the Park.

The Committee recommends that references to the Park in other legislation be reviewed periodically to ensure that consequential legislation remains consistent with the Act.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Drafting and technical amendments

The Committee recommends a number of technical amendments to improve consistency throughout the Bill.

These amendments include—

• correcting cross-references;

• ensuring that references to the Ministry, Director, Board, rangers, and conservation officers are used consistently;

• clarifying the relationship between the Act and the Park management plan;

• clarifying the relationship between permits and concessions;

• improving consistency between enforcement provisions and offence provisions;

• clarifying the operation of temporary closures and emergency directions;

• improving consistency between the principal provisions and the Schedules; and

• making other minor drafting and consequential amendments identified during legislative scrutiny.

The Committee considers that these amendments would improve the workability and clarity of the Bill without altering its fundamental purpose.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Committee view

The Committee considers that the Great Northern National Park Bill represents a significant and long-term commitment to the protection of one of Square Country's most important natural areas.

The Bill provides a comprehensive statutory framework covering conservation, recreation, public access, environmental protection, commercial activities, enforcement, scientific research, infrastructure, funding, pest control, emergency management, and parliamentary accountability.

The Committee recognises that the establishment of the Park will impose responsibilities on the Government to provide adequate funding, staffing, infrastructure, scientific monitoring, and enforcement.

The Committee considers that the effectiveness of the Act will ultimately depend not only on the statutory framework but on its implementation.

The Committee therefore considers that the Government should ensure that the Ministry and Wildlife Conservation Agency have sufficient resources to administer the Park effectively.

The Committee also considers that the public should have access to clear information about Park rules, closures, permits, tracks, campsites, conservation operations, and safety requirements.

The Committee considers that the Park should be managed according to the principle that conservation is the primary purpose, while recognising that responsible public enjoyment, recreation, education, tourism, and scientific research are legitimate and important uses of the Park.

The Committee accordingly recommends that the Bill be passed with the amendments set out in this commentary.

PARTNUMBER: 1

PARTTITLE: Preliminary Provisions

SECTION: 1

HEADING: Title

This Act is the **Great Northern National Park Act 2026**.

SECTION: 2

HEADING: Commencement

This Act comes into force on the day after the date on which it receives royal assent.

SECTION: 3

HEADING: Purpose

The purpose of this Act is—

(a) to establish the Great Northern National Park; and

(b) to provide for the protection, preservation, and enhancement of the natural, scenic, ecological, geological, and recreational values of the Park; and

(c) to provide for the responsible enjoyment of the Park by members of the public; and

(d) to establish a statutory framework for the administration and management of the Park; and

(e) to protect indigenous flora and fauna within the Park; and

(f) to ensure that the Park is managed for the benefit and enjoyment of present and future generations.

SECTION: 4

HEADING: Act binds the Crown

This Act binds the Crown.

SECTION: 5

HEADING: Interpretation

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires—

**Board** means the Great Northern National Park Board established by section 18;

**conservation officer** means a person appointed as a conservation officer under section 64;

**Director-General** means the Director-General of the Ministry;

**Minister** means the Minister responsible for the administration of this Act;

**Ministry** means the Ministry of Environment and Conservation;

**National Park** or **Park** means the Great Northern National Park established by section 7;

**Park management plan** means a management plan prepared and approved under Part 3;

**Park ranger** means a person appointed as a ranger under section 61;

**public road** means a road declared to be a public road under an enactment;

**reserve land** means land forming part of the Park and held for conservation and public recreation purposes;

**vehicle** includes a motor vehicle, motorcycle, bicycle, trailer, and any other mechanically propelled conveyance.

SECTION: 6

HEADING: Application of other enactments

(1) Nothing in this Act limits or affects the operation of any other enactment relating to—

(a) the protection of wildlife;

(b) the protection of forests;

(c) the protection of waterways;

(d) biosecurity;

(e) public health and safety;

(f) the management of public roads; or

(g) the protection of historic or archaeological sites.

(2) Where a provision of this Act is inconsistent with another enactment, this Act prevails to the extent of the inconsistency unless the other enactment expressly provides otherwise.

PARTNUMBER: 2

PARTTITLE: Establishment of the Great Northern National Park

SECTION: 7

HEADING: Establishment of Great Northern National Park

(1) There is established a national park to be known as the **Great Northern National Park**.

(2) The Park is declared to be a national park for the purposes of this Act.

(3) The Park is to be managed in accordance with this Act and the Park management plan.

SECTION: 8

HEADING: Boundaries of Park

(1) The boundaries of the Park are the boundaries described in Schedule 1.

(2) The Minister may, by notice published in the Gazette, correct a clerical or surveying error in the description of the boundaries.

(3) A correction under subsection (2) must not substantially alter the area or character of the Park.

SECTION: 9

HEADING: Addition of land to Park

(1) The Minister may, by notice published in the Gazette, add Crown land to the Park.

(2) Land may be added to the Park only if the Minister is satisfied that the addition—

(a) contributes to the conservation of the natural environment; or

(b) provides significant scenic, ecological, geological, cultural, or recreational value; or

(c) is necessary for the effective management or protection of the Park.

(3) A notice under this section must describe the land added to the Park.

SECTION: 10

HEADING: Removal of land from Park

(1) Land forming part of the Park must not be removed from the Park except by an Act of Parliament.

(2) The Minister must not recommend the removal of land from the Park unless the Minister is satisfied that—

(a) there is an exceptional public interest in the removal; and

(b) there is no reasonably practicable alternative; and

(c) the environmental consequences have been fully assessed.

SECTION: 11

HEADING: Status of Park land

(1) Land forming part of the Park is held for the purposes of this Act.

(2) Park land must be managed primarily for the preservation of its natural and scenic values.

(3) No part of the Park may be sold, transferred, or otherwise disposed of except as expressly authorised by this Act or another Act of Parliament.

SECTION: 12

HEADING: National park status

The declaration of the Park constitutes a permanent statutory recognition that the land within its boundaries is of national importance for conservation, recreation, scientific research, education, and the enjoyment of the public.

SECTION: 13

HEADING: Objects of management

The objects of management of the Park are—

(a) to preserve its indigenous ecosystems;

(b) to protect indigenous species and their habitats;

(c) to preserve landscapes and geological features;

(d) to protect rivers, lakes, wetlands, springs, and other waterways;

(e) to maintain the ecological integrity of the Park;

(f) to provide opportunities for public recreation consistent with conservation;

(g) to provide for scientific research and education;

(h) to protect sites of historic or archaeological significance; and

(i) to prevent activities that would permanently or materially degrade the Park.

SECTION: 14

HEADING: Precautionary principle

In making decisions under this Act, the Minister, Board, and officers administering the Park must take a precautionary approach where there is a risk of serious or irreversible environmental damage.

SECTION: 15

HEADING: Public ownership

(1) The Park is held for the benefit of the people of Square Country.

(2) No person acquires a private right of ownership over any part of the Park merely by occupying, using, or improving that part of the Park.

SECTION: 16

HEADING: Official name

The official name of the Park is **Great Northern National Park**.

(2) The Minister may assign names to tracks, facilities, rivers, lakes, peaks, valleys, campsites, and other features within the Park.

PARTNUMBER: 3

PARTTITLE: Administration and Governance

SECTION: 17

HEADING: Administration of Park

(1) The Ministry is responsible for the administration of this Act.

(2) The Ministry must ensure that the Park is managed in accordance with the purposes and objects of this Act.

SECTION: 18

HEADING: Great Northern National Park Board

(1) There is established a body called the **Great Northern National Park Board**.

(2) The Board is responsible for advising the Minister and the Ministry on the management and protection of the Park.

SECTION: 19

HEADING: Membership of Board

(1) The Board consists of not fewer than 7 and not more than 11 members appointed by the Minister.

(2) In appointing members, the Minister must seek to ensure that the Board collectively possesses knowledge and experience in—

(a) conservation;

(b) ecology;

(c) forestry;

(d) recreation;

(e) tourism;

(f) environmental science;

(g) local government;

(h) land management; and

(i) public administration.

SECTION: 20

HEADING: Term of appointment

(1) A member of the Board holds office for a term of 4 years.

(2) A member may be reappointed.

(3) A member must not serve more than 3 consecutive terms unless the Minister considers that exceptional circumstances justify further appointment.

SECTION: 21

HEADING: Functions of Board

The functions of the Board are—

(a) to advise the Minister on the Park;

(b) to review the Park management plan;

(c) to advise on major development proposals;

(d) to promote public understanding of conservation;

(e) to monitor the condition of the Park;

(f) to advise on visitor management;

(g) to review reports concerning environmental damage; and

(h) to perform any other function conferred on it by this Act.

SECTION: 22

HEADING: Meetings of Board

(1) The Board must meet at least 4 times in every calendar year.

(2) The Board may regulate its own procedure, subject to this Act.

(3) The Board must keep minutes of its meetings.

SECTION: 23

HEADING: Annual report

(1) The Board must provide the Minister with an annual report concerning the administration and condition of the Park.

(2) The report must include—

(a) the financial position of the Park;

(b) visitor numbers;

(c) major conservation projects;

(d) significant environmental incidents;

(e) enforcement activity;

(f) the condition of major infrastructure; and

(g) recommendations for the following year.

SECTION: 24

HEADING: Director of Great Northern National Park

(1) The Minister may appoint a Director of Great Northern National Park.

(2) The Director is responsible for the day-to-day administration of the Park.

(3) The Director must report to the Director-General.

SECTION: 25

HEADING: Duties of Director

The Director must—

(a) implement the Park management plan;

(b) supervise Park staff;

(c) maintain Park infrastructure;

(d) protect the natural environment;

(e) facilitate lawful public access;

(f) enforce this Act and regulations made under it; and

(g) provide information and advice to the Minister and Board.

PARTNUMBER: 4

PARTTITLE: Park Management Plan

SECTION: 26

HEADING: Requirement for management plan

(1) The Director must prepare a Park management plan.

(2) The plan must provide for the long-term management of the Park.

(3) The plan must be reviewed at least once every 10 years.

SECTION: 27

HEADING: Contents of management plan

The management plan must include provisions relating to—

(a) conservation objectives;

(b) biodiversity protection;

(c) fire management;

(d) pest and invasive species control;

(e) visitor management;

(f) tracks and roads;

(g) camping;

(h) hunting and fishing;

(i) scientific research;

(j) commercial activities;

(k) emergency management;

(l) waste management;

(m) protection of waterways;

(n) protection of historic sites;

(o) climate and environmental resilience; and

(p) any other matter considered necessary for the effective management of the Park.

SECTION: 28

HEADING: Public consultation

(1) Before approving a management plan, the Minister must ensure that reasonable opportunities are provided for members of the public to make submissions.

(2) The Minister must consider submissions received.

SECTION: 29

HEADING: Approval of management plan

(1) A management plan has no effect until approved by the Minister.

(2) The Minister may approve the plan with or without amendments.

(3) The approved plan must be published on the Ministry's website and made available for public inspection.

SECTION: 30

HEADING: Effect of management plan

(1) The Director and every officer administering the Park must have regard to the management plan when exercising powers under this Act.

(2) A person undertaking an activity under a permit or concession must comply with the applicable provisions of the management plan.

PARTNUMBER: 5

PARTTITLE: Conservation and Protection of the Park

SECTION: 31

HEADING: General duty to protect Park

Every person entering or undertaking an activity within the Park must take all reasonable steps to avoid unnecessary damage to the natural environment.

SECTION: 32

HEADING: Protection of indigenous vegetation

(1) A person must not cut, remove, damage, destroy, uproot, or otherwise interfere with indigenous vegetation within the Park except—

(a) under a permit issued under this Act; or

(b) as reasonably necessary for public safety or emergency management.

(2) Subsection (1) applies whether or not the vegetation is alive.

SECTION: 33

HEADING: Protection of wildlife

(1) A person must not hunt, capture, kill, injure, disturb, or deliberately interfere with indigenous wildlife in the Park except as authorised by this Act or another enactment.

(2) A person must not knowingly destroy or interfere with the nest, den, breeding place, or habitat of protected wildlife.

SECTION: 34

HEADING: Introduction of animals

(1) A person must not release or introduce an animal into the Park without a permit.

(2) This section does not apply to an animal being lawfully transported through the Park on a public road.

SECTION: 35

HEADING: Introduced plants and organisms

A person must not knowingly introduce a plant, seed, fungus, microorganism, pest, or other organism into the Park where the introduction is reasonably likely to harm the natural environment.

SECTION: 36

HEADING: Fire

(1) A person must not light, maintain, or use a fire within the Park except in—

(a) a designated fireplace;

(b) an area specified by the Director; or

(c) circumstances authorised by a permit.

(2) The Director may prohibit all fires in any part of the Park where conditions create a significant risk of wildfire.

SECTION: 37

HEADING: Firearms

(1) A person must not discharge a firearm within the Park except—

(a) for lawful hunting in an area where hunting is permitted;

(b) for the destruction of an injured animal where reasonably necessary; or

(c) for a purpose authorised by the Director.

(2) The Director may prohibit the possession or use of firearms in specified areas.

SECTION: 38

HEADING: Pollution

A person must not discharge, deposit, release, or allow to escape into the Park any substance that is reasonably likely to cause significant environmental harm.

SECTION: 39

HEADING: Waterways

(1) A person must not knowingly pollute or materially alter a river, stream, lake, wetland, spring, or other natural waterway within the Park.

(2) No person may construct a dam, diversion, drainage system, or other substantial obstruction in a waterway within the Park without a permit.

SECTION: 40

HEADING: Mining and extraction

(1) No person may undertake mining, quarrying, drilling, gravel extraction, or the extraction of minerals within the Park.

(2) Subsection (1) does not prevent the removal of material where authorised by the Director for—

(a) track construction;

(b) erosion control;

(c) public safety; or

(d) restoration of the Park.

SECTION: 41

HEADING: Commercial forestry

Commercial forestry operations must not be undertaken within the Park.

SECTION: 42

HEADING: Waste

(1) A person must not leave rubbish, waste, equipment, or other material in the Park except in a place provided for that purpose.

(2) A person who brings material into the Park must, so far as reasonably practicable, remove it when leaving.

SECTION: 43

HEADING: Damage to natural features

A person must not intentionally or recklessly damage, deface, remove, or interfere with any natural feature of the Park.

SECTION: 44

HEADING: Cave and geological protection

A person must not damage or remove rocks, fossils, caves, mineral formations, or other geological features within the Park except as authorised by a permit.

PARTNUMBER: 6

PARTTITLE: Public Access and Recreation

SECTION: 45

HEADING: Right of public access

(1) Members of the public may enter and travel through the Park for lawful recreational purposes.

(2) The right of access under subsection (1) is subject to—

(a) this Act;

(b) regulations;

(c) the Park management plan;

(d) reasonable directions of Park officers; and

(e) temporary closures made under this Act.

SECTION: 46

HEADING: Tracks

(1) The Director must establish and maintain a network of public walking tracks appropriate to the character and environmental sensitivity of the Park.

(2) The Director may classify tracks according to their difficulty, environmental sensitivity, or permitted uses.

SECTION: 47

HEADING: Vehicles

(1) A person must not operate a motor vehicle anywhere in the Park other than—

(a) on a public road;

(b) on a designated vehicle track; or

(c) with the authority of the Director.

(2) The Director may prohibit vehicles from any track where necessary for conservation or safety.

SECTION: 48

HEADING: Cycling

The Director may designate tracks or areas for cycling and may prohibit cycling in areas where it would cause unreasonable environmental damage or interfere with public safety.

SECTION: 49

HEADING: Camping

(1) A person must not camp in the Park except—

(a) at a designated campsite; or

(b) with a permit issued under this Act.

(2) The Director may establish different classes of campsite.

(3) The Director may impose reasonable conditions relating to the use of campsites.

SECTION: 50

HEADING: Permanent structures

A person must not erect a permanent structure within the Park without the written authority of the Director.

SECTION: 51

HEADING: Dogs and domestic animals

(1) A person must not bring a dog into the Park except in an area designated for dogs or with the authority of the Director.

(2) A person must not allow a domestic animal to roam freely within the Park.

(3) This section does not prevent the use of animals by Park staff for management purposes.

SECTION: 52

HEADING: Drones

(1) A person must not operate an unmanned aircraft within the Park without a permit.

(2) A permit may be issued for—

(a) scientific research;

(b) conservation;

(c) emergency response;

(d) search and rescue;

(e) approved commercial purposes; or

(f) other purposes considered appropriate by the Director.

SECTION: 53

HEADING: Public events

(1) A person must not organise a sporting event, concert, festival, commercial gathering, or other organised public event within the Park without a permit.

(2) The Director must consider the likely environmental and recreational effects of the event before issuing a permit.

SECTION: 54

HEADING: Visitor safety

The Director may issue directions concerning—

(a) dangerous weather;

(b) flooding;

(c) fire;

(d) landslides;

(e) dangerous wildlife;

(f) track conditions; and

(g) any other threat to public safety.

PARTNUMBER: 7

PARTTITLE: Permits, Concessions, and Commercial Activities

SECTION: 55

HEADING: Requirement for permit

A person must not undertake an activity for which this Act requires a permit unless a permit has been issued and remains in force.

SECTION: 56

HEADING: Application for permit

An application for a permit must—

(a) be made in the prescribed form;

(b) identify the proposed activity;

(c) identify the area in which the activity is proposed;

(d) state the proposed duration; and

(e) contain any other information reasonably required by the Director.

SECTION: 57

HEADING: Grant of permit

The Director may grant a permit if satisfied that the activity—

(a) is consistent with the purposes of this Act;

(b) is consistent with the management plan;

(c) will not cause unacceptable environmental damage; and

(d) can be adequately monitored.

SECTION: 58

HEADING: Conditions of permit

A permit may contain conditions relating to—

(a) location;

(b) hours of operation;

(c) environmental protection;

(d) waste disposal;

(e) public safety;

(f) insurance;

(g) restoration of affected land;

(h) payment of fees; and

(i) any other matter reasonably necessary.

SECTION: 59

HEADING: Suspension and cancellation

The Director may suspend or cancel a permit if—

(a) the holder breaches a condition;

(b) the activity causes unforeseen environmental harm;

(c) the holder provided materially false information; or

(d) suspension or cancellation is necessary for public safety.

SECTION: 60

HEADING: Commercial activities

A person must not conduct a commercial activity within the Park unless authorised under a permit or concession.

SECTION: 61

HEADING: Concessions

(1) The Minister may grant a concession for the operation of facilities or services within the Park.

(2) A concession may relate to—

(a) accommodation;

(b) guided tours;

(c) transport;

(d) food services;

(e) visitor facilities;

(f) educational services; or

(g) other activities compatible with the purposes of the Park.

(3) A concession must not confer ownership of Park land.

PARTNUMBER: 8

PARTTITLE: Park Officers and Enforcement

SECTION: 62

HEADING: Park rangers

(1) The Director may appoint persons as Park rangers.

(2) A ranger must carry identification in the prescribed form.

SECTION: 63

HEADING: Functions of rangers

A ranger may—

(a) patrol the Park;

(b) provide information and assistance to visitors;

(c) enforce this Act;

(d) issue directions authorised by this Act;

(e) investigate suspected offences;

(f) protect Park property; and

(g) assist in emergencies.

SECTION: 64

HEADING: Conservation officers

The Minister may appoint conservation officers for the purposes of enforcing this Act.

SECTION: 65

HEADING: Power to require information

(1) An enforcement officer may require a person whom the officer reasonably suspects has committed an offence under this Act to provide the person's name and address.

(2) A person must comply with a lawful requirement under this section.

SECTION: 66

HEADING: Direction to leave Park

(1) An enforcement officer may direct a person to leave the Park or a specified part of the Park if the officer reasonably believes that—

(a) the person is committing an offence;

(b) the person is creating a danger to themselves or another person; or

(c) the person's conduct is causing significant environmental harm.

(2) A person must comply with a lawful direction under this section.

SECTION: 67

HEADING: Seizure

An enforcement officer may seize any thing if the officer reasonably believes that the thing—

(a) has been used in the commission of an offence; or

(b) constitutes evidence of an offence under this Act.

SECTION: 68

HEADING: Search of abandoned property

An enforcement officer may inspect or search property apparently abandoned within the Park for the purpose of determining—

(a) its owner;

(b) whether it constitutes a danger; or

(c) whether it is evidence of an offence.

SECTION: 69

HEADING: Obstruction of officers

A person must not intentionally obstruct, hinder, threaten, or assault an officer exercising a function under this Act.

PARTNUMBER: 9

PARTTITLE: Offences and Penalties

SECTION: 70

HEADING: General offence

A person commits an offence who knowingly or recklessly contravenes a provision of this Act or a condition of a permit.

SECTION: 71

HEADING: Serious environmental offence

A person commits a serious environmental offence if the person intentionally causes substantial or permanent damage to the natural environment of the Park.

SECTION: 72

HEADING: Penalty for individuals

A person convicted of an offence under this Act is liable to—

(a) in the case of a minor offence, a fine not exceeding ₴5,000; or

(b) in the case of a serious environmental offence, a fine not exceeding ₴100,000 or imprisonment for a term not exceeding 2 years, or both.

SECTION: 73

HEADING: Penalty for bodies corporate

A body corporate convicted of an offence under this Act is liable to a fine not exceeding—

(a) ₴25,000 for a minor offence; or

(b) ₴500,000 for a serious environmental offence.

SECTION: 74

HEADING: Continuing offences

Where an offence continues for more than 1 day, the offender commits a further offence for each day during which the offence continues.

SECTION: 75

HEADING: Liability for damage

A person who causes damage to the Park may be required to—

(a) restore the affected area;

(b) remove structures or materials;

(c) undertake rehabilitation work; or

(d) pay the reasonable cost of restoration.

SECTION: 76

HEADING: Court orders

On convicting a person of an offence, a court may order—

(a) forfeiture of equipment used in the commission of the offence;

(b) restoration of damaged land;

(c) payment of costs incurred by the Crown;

(d) removal of an unlawful structure; or

(e) any other remedy authorised by law.

PARTNUMBER: 10

PARTTITLE: Fire, Emergency, and Closure Powers

SECTION: 77

HEADING: Temporary closure

(1) The Director may temporarily close all or part of the Park if satisfied that closure is necessary because of—

(a) fire;

(b) flooding;

(c) severe weather;

(d) disease;

(e) environmental damage;

(f) dangerous wildlife;

(g) search and rescue operations; or

(h) another significant threat to public safety or the environment.

(2) A closure must be publicised by reasonable means.

SECTION: 78

HEADING: Emergency directions

During an emergency, the Director or a ranger may give reasonable directions to persons within the Park.

SECTION: 79

HEADING: Evacuation

A person must comply with a lawful direction to evacuate an area of the Park.

SECTION: 80

HEADING: Emergency works

The Director may undertake works necessary to protect life, property, or the environment without obtaining a permit otherwise required by this Act.

PARTNUMBER: 11

PARTTITLE: Scientific Research and Education

SECTION: 81

HEADING: Scientific research

(1) The Director may issue permits for scientific research within the Park.

(2) A research permit may authorise activities that would otherwise be prohibited if the Director is satisfied that the research is justified and appropriately controlled.

SECTION: 82

HEADING: Research conditions

A research permit may require—

(a) the submission of research results to the Ministry;

(b) the protection of specimens;

(c) the restoration of research sites;

(d) restrictions on collection;

(e) monitoring of environmental effects; and

(f) compliance with recognised scientific standards.

SECTION: 83

HEADING: Educational activities

The Director must encourage educational use of the Park where such use is consistent with conservation.

SECTION: 84

HEADING: Visitor centres

The Ministry may establish visitor centres, information facilities, museums, educational facilities, and other public amenities within the Park.

PARTNUMBER: 12

PARTTITLE: Hunting, Fishing, and Natural Resource Use

SECTION: 85

HEADING: Hunting

(1) Hunting is prohibited within areas designated by the Minister.

(2) The Minister may designate specified areas where controlled hunting may occur.

(3) Any hunting permitted under this section must be carried out in accordance with regulations and the management plan.

SECTION: 86

HEADING: Fishing

(1) Fishing may be permitted in specified waterways within the Park.

(2) The Minister may establish restrictions relating to—

(a) species;

(b) seasons;

(c) catch limits;

(d) methods of fishing; and

(e) areas where fishing is permitted.

SECTION: 87

HEADING: Protection of endangered species

The Minister may prohibit hunting, fishing, collection, or other interference with any species considered threatened or endangered.

SECTION: 88

HEADING: Collection of natural materials

A person must not remove plants, rocks, fossils, soil, sand, timber, or other natural materials from the Park except as authorised by permit or regulations.

PARTNUMBER: 13

PARTTITLE: Heritage and Archaeological Protection

SECTION: 89

HEADING: Protection of historic sites

A person must not damage, alter, remove, or interfere with a historic structure, monument, archaeological site, or other place of historic significance within the Park.

SECTION: 90

HEADING: Discovery of archaeological material

A person who discovers an object or site that may be of archaeological significance must—

(a) cease disturbing the object or site; and

(b) notify the Ministry as soon as reasonably practicable.

SECTION: 91

HEADING: Register of protected sites

The Ministry must maintain a register of historic, archaeological, geological, and other significant sites within the Park.

PARTNUMBER: 14

PARTTITLE: Infrastructure and Development

SECTION: 92

HEADING: Park infrastructure

The Ministry may construct and maintain—

(a) roads;

(b) bridges;

(c) walking tracks;

(d) huts;

(e) visitor centres;

(f) toilets;

(g) campsites;

(h) signs;

(i) communication facilities; and

(j) other infrastructure necessary for the administration and enjoyment of the Park.

SECTION: 93

HEADING: Environmental assessment

Before approving a major development within the Park, the Director must ensure that an assessment is undertaken of—

(a) effects on biodiversity;

(b) effects on waterways;

(c) effects on landscape values;

(d) effects on recreation;

(e) effects on historic sites; and

(f) cumulative environmental effects.

SECTION: 94

HEADING: Development prohibited where unacceptable

The Director must not approve a development if satisfied that its effects would be inconsistent with the fundamental conservation purposes of the Park.

SECTION: 95

HEADING: Restoration

Where a temporary facility or structure is authorised within the Park, the person responsible must restore the affected land when the activity ends unless the Director determines otherwise.

PARTNUMBER: 15

PARTTITLE: Funding and Financial Administration

SECTION: 96

HEADING: Great Northern National Park Fund

(1) There is established a fund called the **Great Northern National Park Fund**.

(2) The Fund is administered by the Ministry.

SECTION: 97

HEADING: Money paid into Fund

The following must be paid into the Fund—

(a) appropriations made by Parliament;

(b) fees and charges collected under this Act;

(c) concession payments;

(d) donations and grants accepted by the Ministry; and

(e) any other money authorised to be paid into the Fund.

SECTION: 98

HEADING: Application of Fund

Money in the Fund may be applied only for—

(a) conservation;

(b) Park infrastructure;

(c) scientific research;

(d) visitor facilities;

(e) environmental restoration;

(f) ranger services;

(g) fire management;

(h) pest control; and

(i) administration of the Park.

SECTION: 99

HEADING: Fees

The Minister may prescribe fees for—

(a) permits;

(b) concessions;

(c) camping;

(d) commercial activities;

(e) scientific research; and

(f) other services provided under this Act.

SECTION: 100

HEADING: No general admission fee

No person may be charged a general admission fee merely for entering the Park on foot unless authorised by an Act of Parliament.

PARTNUMBER: 16

PARTTITLE: Pests, Invasive Species, and Restoration

SECTION: 101

HEADING: Pest management

The Director must establish programmes for the control or eradication of invasive species and pests that threaten the ecological integrity of the Park.

SECTION: 102

HEADING: Pest control operations

The Director may undertake—

(a) trapping;

(b) poisoning;

(c) fencing;

(d) removal;

(e) biological control; and

(f) other methods approved under the management plan.

SECTION: 103

HEADING: Notice of pest control

Where reasonably practicable, the Ministry must give public notice of pest control operations that may affect visitors.

SECTION: 104

HEADING: Ecological restoration

The Ministry may undertake restoration projects for—

(a) degraded forests;

(b) wetlands;

(c) waterways;

(d) grasslands;

(e) habitats of threatened species; and

(f) other damaged ecosystems.

SECTION: 105

HEADING: Restoration agreements

The Ministry may enter into agreements with public bodies, scientific institutions, community organisations, and other persons for the restoration of the Park.

PARTNUMBER: 17

PARTTITLE: Regulations and Administrative Matters

SECTION: 106

HEADING: Regulations

The Governor-General may, by Order in Council, make regulations for the purposes of this Act.

SECTION: 107

HEADING: Matters that may be prescribed

Without limiting section 106, regulations may provide for—

(a) camping;

(b) fees;

(c) permits;

(d) concessions;

(e) hunting;

(f) fishing;

(g) vehicles;

(h) fires;

(i) dogs and domestic animals;

(j) drones;

(k) commercial activities;

(l) public events;

(m) waste;

(n) scientific research;

(o) conservation activities;

(p) offences against regulations; and

(q) any other matter necessary for giving effect to this Act.

SECTION: 108

HEADING: Bylaws

(1) The Minister may make bylaws for the orderly management of particular areas of the Park.

(2) A bylaw may—

(a) restrict access to a particular area;

(b) regulate visitor behaviour;

(c) regulate vehicles;

(d) establish camping requirements;

(e) protect sensitive environmental areas; or

(f) regulate any other matter consistent with this Act.

SECTION: 109

HEADING: Publication of bylaws

Every bylaw made under this Act must be published in the Gazette and made publicly available.

SECTION: 110

HEADING: Review of Act

The Minister must review the operation of this Act at least once every 10 years and report the results of the review to Parliament.

SECTION: 111

HEADING: Annual state of the Park report

The Minister must, once every calendar year, present to Parliament a report on the state of the Great Northern National Park.

The report must include—

(a) the ecological condition of the Park;

(b) significant environmental threats;

(c) visitor numbers;

(d) major developments;

(e) enforcement activity;

(f) expenditure;

(g) conservation achievements; and

(h) priorities for the following year.

PARTNUMBER: 18

PARTTITLE: Miscellaneous and Transitional Provisions

SECTION: 112

HEADING: Existing rights and interests

Nothing in this Act extinguishes a lawful right or interest existing immediately before the commencement of this Act unless the right or interest is expressly extinguished by this Act.

SECTION: 113

HEADING: Existing agreements

An agreement relating to land incorporated into the Park continues in force according to its terms unless—

(a) the agreement is inconsistent with this Act; or

(b) the agreement is terminated in accordance with its terms.

SECTION: 114

HEADING: Existing facilities

A facility existing within the boundaries of the Park immediately before commencement may continue to operate subject to—

(a) this Act;

(b) regulations;

(c) the management plan; and

(d) any conditions imposed by the Director.

SECTION: 115

HEADING: Transitional permits

A permit or authorisation relating to land incorporated into the Park remains valid for 12 months after commencement unless earlier cancelled or replaced.

SECTION: 116

HEADING: Crown land transferred to Park

Any Crown land identified in Schedule 1 as forming part of the Park is, on commencement, declared to constitute part of the Great Northern National Park.

SECTION: 117

HEADING: Protection of existing public roads

A public road within the boundaries of the Park remains a public road unless lawfully closed under another enactment.

SECTION: 118

HEADING: Saving of existing offences

Nothing in this Act affects proceedings for an offence committed before the commencement of this Act.

SECTION: 119

HEADING: Delegation

(1) The Minister, Director-General, Director, or Board may delegate any function or power under this Act to an employee of the Ministry or another person who is appropriately qualified.

(2) A delegation must be in writing.

(3) A person exercising a delegated power must comply with any conditions of the delegation.

SECTION: 120

HEADING: Protection from liability

A person is not personally liable for anything done or omitted to be done in good faith in the exercise of a power or function under this Act.

SECTION: 121

HEADING: Crown not liable for certain loss

The Crown is not liable for loss suffered by a person merely because the person—

(a) enters the Park;

(b) uses a track or facility;

(c) relies on information provided by a Park officer; or

(d) is unable to undertake an activity because an area of the Park is closed.

SECTION: 122

HEADING: Regulations not to authorise inconsistency

No regulation or bylaw made under this Act may authorise conduct that is inconsistent with the fundamental conservation purposes of the Park.

SECTION: 123

HEADING: Repeal and consequential amendments

The enactments specified in Schedule 2 are amended or repealed to the extent specified in that Schedule.

SECTION: 124

HEADING: Crown land schedule

The land described in Schedule 1 is declared to be part of the Great Northern National Park on the commencement of this Act.

SECTION: 125

HEADING: Parliamentary control

No provision of this Act authorises the permanent disposal of land forming part of the Great Northern National Park.

SECTION: 126

HEADING: Review of Park boundaries

(1) The Minister must review the boundaries of the Park at least once every 15 years.

(2) The purpose of the review is to determine whether additional land should be incorporated into the Park.

(3) The Minister must consult the Board before completing a review.

SECTION: 127

HEADING: Enlargement of Park

The Minister may recommend to Parliament that additional land be incorporated into the Park where the land has significant—

(a) ecological value;

(b) scenic value;

(c) geological value;

(d) recreational value;

(e) conservation value; or

(f) cultural or historical value.

SECTION: 128

HEADING: Act to be administered

This Act is to be administered by the Ministry for the Environment; and Wildlife Conservation Agency.

SCHEDULENUMBER: 1

SCHEDULETITLE: Description of Great Northern National Park

SCHEDULESECTION: 1

(1) The Great Northern National Park comprises the areas of Crown land identified on the official survey plans deposited with the Registrar-General of Lands for the purposes of this Act.
(2) The boundaries of the Park are to be shown on an official map maintained by the Ministry.
(3) The official map must identify—
(a) the external boundaries of the Park;
(b) public roads;
(c) waterways;
(d) designated recreational areas;
(e) restricted conservation areas; and
(f) other areas required for the effective administration of the Park.
(4) Where there is an inconsistency between a general description of the Park and the official survey plan, the official survey plan prevails.

SCHEDULENUMBER: 2

SCHEDULETITLE: Consequential Amendments

SCHEDULESECTION: 1

(1) The Conservation Act is amended by inserting, after the provisions relating to national parks, a reference to the Great Northern National Park.
(2) The Land Management Act is amended so that land forming part of the Great Northern National Park is subject to the provisions of this Act.
(3) The Public Reserves Act is amended to recognise the Great Northern National Park as a national park established by Act of Parliament.
(4) Any reference in an enactment to a national park administered by the Ministry is taken, where the context permits, to include the Great Northern National Park.
