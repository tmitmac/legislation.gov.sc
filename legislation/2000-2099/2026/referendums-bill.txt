COMMENTARYHEADING: Commentary

COMMENTARYSECTION: Recommendation
The Justice and Governance Committee has examined the Bill and recommends by majority that it be passed.

COMMENTARYSECTION: About the bill as introduced
The Referendums Bill (the **Bill**) seeks to repeal Part 15 of the *Electoral Act 1986* and establish a permanent legislative framework for the conduct of referendums in Square Country.

The bill is intended to provide a clear and consistent set of rules governing the administration of referendums, while ensuring that the conduct of a referendum is integrated with the country’s wider electoral system. The framework is designed to apply to referendums held on matters referred to electors by Parliament or otherwise authorised by legislation.

The bill would transfer the principal statutory framework for the conduct of referendums from Part 15 of the *Electoral Act 1986* into a dedicated Referendums Act. This would make the rules relating to referendums easier to identify and administer, while allowing the electoral legislation governing general elections to continue to apply where appropriate.

The bill provides for the **Electoral Commission** to administer referendums and establishes rules concerning the appointment and functions of electoral officials, polling places, voter eligibility, referendum voting papers, voting procedures, the counting and declaration of referendum votes, and the publication of official results.

The bill would also establish procedures for questioning the validity or result of a referendum, including mechanisms for applications to the courts where an alleged irregularity may have materially affected the outcome. It provides for appropriate offences and penalties in connection with the conduct of referendums.

A significant purpose of the bill is to provide a framework that can be used for different types of referendums without requiring a separate procedural regime to be enacted for each individual referendum. The substantive question put to electors would continue to be determined by the legislation or other authority establishing the referendum, while the bill would provide the machinery through which that referendum is conducted.

The bill also provides for referendum advertising and campaigning to be subject to appropriate controls. These provisions are intended to promote transparency around referendum promoters and expenditure and to provide safeguards against excessive or unauthorised expenditure during a regulated period.

The framework broadly follows the principles and administrative arrangements used for parliamentary elections. This approach would allow existing electoral infrastructure, personnel, polling places and administrative systems to be used for referendums wherever practicable. It would also provide voters and campaign participants with a familiar and consistent set of electoral procedures.

The bill accordingly seeks to replace the existing referendum provisions in Part 15 of the *Electoral Act 1986* with a dedicated and comprehensive legislative framework, providing greater clarity, consistency and certainty for the conduct of referendums in Square Country.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Legislative scrutiny
The Committee has examined the Bill to ensure that its proposed referendum framework is clear, workable, and consistent with the wider electoral system of Square Country.

Key matters considered include:

• Repeal of Part 15: Whether the repeal of Part 15 of the Electoral Act 1986 appropriately removes the existing referendum framework.
• Electoral Commission: Whether the Commission has appropriate powers and responsibilities to administer referendums.
• Voting procedures: Whether the proposed rules provide clear and accessible procedures for voters.
• Campaigning and advertising: Whether appropriate safeguards are provided for referendum campaigns and expenditure.
• Offences and enforcement: Whether the proposed offences and penalties are proportionate and enforceable.
• Judicial oversight: Whether sufficient mechanisms exist to challenge referendum irregularities and the validity of results.

The bill is intended to provide a clear, standalone legislative framework while maintaining consistency with established electoral law and administrative practice.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Our process and proposed amendments

Our process and proposed amendments

The bill has been considered through the legislative scrutiny process, with particular attention given to whether the proposed referendum framework is clear, practical, and consistent with existing electoral law.

Following this consideration, the following amendments are proposed:

• **Clarifying the Electoral Commission’s role** in administering and supervising referendums.
• **Improving drafting consistency** between the proposed Act and existing electoral legislation.
• **Clarifying referendum procedures**, including voting, counting, and the declaration of results.
• **Strengthening transparency requirements** for referendum advertising and campaign expenditure.
• **Clarifying transitional provisions** following the repeal of Part 15 of the *Electoral Act 1986*.
• **Correcting minor drafting and consequential amendments** required as a result of establishing the new framework.

These amendments are intended to improve the clarity and operation of the bill without changing its fundamental purpose of establishing a dedicated legislative framework for referendums in Square Country.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Square Country Greens differing view

The Square Country Greens do not support this bill. Throughout the committee process we have seen time and again the government removing the powers of the Electoral Commission, in favour of their own rules and regulations that undermine the freeness, fairness, and independence of electoral matters in Square Country.

We believe that Part 15 of the *Electoral Act 1986* is currently perfectly capable of providing the legal framework for which referendums are held under in Square Country. Not to mention the fact that referendums do not take place in Square Country often—the last one being in 1986 for the transition to Mixed-Member Proportional (MMP).

We also believe that this bill opens the door to more campaigning, making it easier to sway voters in a particular direction when it comes to referendum voting. Whilst this was never illegal, it was heavily regulated under the *Electoral Act 1986*. Now, a number of critical checks, balances, and rules have been abolished, opening the door to significant interference in genuine referendums.

It is for these reasons that the Square Country Greens do not support this bill.

COMMENTARYSECTION: Innovation Party differing view

The Innovation Party does not support this bill. We believe that the Referendums Bill is not neccessary and does not address the key issues around party donations, lobbying, and opening voting rights to non-citizens.

It is for this reason that the Innovation Party does not support this bill.

PARTNUMBER: 1
PARTTITLE: Preliminary provisions

SECTION: 1

HEADING: Title

This Act is the **Referendums Act 2026**.

SECTION: 2

HEADING: Commencement

This Act comes into force the day after it receives Royal assent.

SECTION: 3

HEADING: Purpose

The purpose of this act is to—
(a) repeal Part 15 (Referendums) of the [[Act:Electoral Act 1986]];
(b) to provide for the conduct of referendums in Square Country;
(c) to ensure that referendums are administered fairly and impartially;
(d) to regulate campaigning and advertising relating to referendum questions;
(e) to provide for the counting and certification of referendum results;
(f) to distinguish between binding and indicative referendums.

SECTION: 4

HEADING: Interpretation

1. In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires—
**advertisement** means any form of communication, whether published in print, broadcast, displayed electronically, distributed physically, or communicated by any other means, that is intended to influence the outcome of a referendum or encourage or discourage voting on a referendum question;
**approved form** means a form approved by the Electoral Commission for the purposes of this Act;
**campaign** means any organised activity undertaken for the purpose of promoting or opposing a referendum question or a particular response to that question;
**campaign expenditure** means expenditure incurred for the purpose of promoting or opposing a referendum question and includes expenditure on advertising, publications, events, broadcasting, digital communications, opinion polling, and any other prescribed matter;
**campaign organisation** means a body, association, incorporated society, company, political party, or other organisation that is registered under this Act to campaign in relation to a referendum;
**candidate** has the same meaning as in the [[Act:Electoral Act 1986]] where the context requires;
**Chief Electoral Officer** means the Chief Electoral Officer appointed under the [[Act:Electoral Act 1986]];
**Commission** means the Electoral Commission established under the [[Act:Electoral Act 1986]];
**count** includes the preliminary count, official count, recount, and any verification of referendum votes conducted under this Act;
**declaration of the result** means the formal declaration of the official result of a referendum by the Electoral Commission;
**elector** means a person whose name appears on an electoral roll and who is entitled to vote in a referendum under this Act;
**electoral district** means an electoral district established under the Electoral Act 1986;
**electoral roll** means a roll of electors prepared and maintained under the Electoral Act 1986;
**electronic communication** includes communication by email, internet website, social media platform, text message, mobile application, or any other digital means;
**government agency** means any Ministry, department, Crown entity, statutory authority, local authority, or other public body established by or under an enactment;
**Minister** means the Minister of Justice & Corrections;
**Ministry** means the Ministry of Justice & Corrections;
**official count** means the final count of referendum votes undertaken by the Electoral Commission under this Act;
**official result** means the result declared following completion of the official count;
**Parliament** means His Majesty's Parliament of Square Country;
**person** includes an individual, corporation, incorporated society, partnership, trust, government agency, political party, or any other legal entity;
**polling day** means the day appointed for the holding of a referendum;
**polling place** means a place appointed by the Electoral Commission at which referendum votes may be cast;
**postal vote** means a referendum vote cast by post in accordance with this Act;
**prescribed** means prescribed by regulations made under this Act;
**question** means the proposition, proposal, or matter submitted to electors for determination at a referendum and includes any explanatory wording approved under this Act;
**referendum** means a poll of electors conducted under this Act for the purpose of determining the opinion or decision of electors on one or more specified questions;
**referendum period** means the period beginning on the day a referendum is formally announced and ending on the day the official result is declared;
**referendum vote** means a vote cast by an elector at a referendum conducted under this Act;
**regulations** means regulations made under this Act;
**returning officer** means a returning officer appointed under the Electoral Act 1986 or under this Act;
**scrutineer** means a person appointed under this Act to observe voting, counting, recounting, or other referendum procedures;
**special vote** means a referendum vote cast under the special voting provisions of this Act;
**tied result** means a result in which the number of valid votes cast in favour of each available response is equal;
**valid vote** means a referendum vote that is accepted as valid under this Act;
**voter** means an elector who is entitled to cast, or who casts, a referendum vote.
2. A reference in this Act to voting includes voting in person, by post, by advance voting, by special voting, by online voting, or by any other method authorised by this Act.
3. Unless expressly provided otherwise, words and expressions defined in the Electoral Act 1986 have the same meanings in this Act.
4. A reference in this Act to a referendum includes—
(a) a binding referendum; and
(b) a non-binding or indicative referendum; and
(c) a constitutional referendum; and
(d) a citizens-initiated referendum; and
(e) any other class of referendum authorised by or under an enactment.
5. In interpreting this Act, regard must be had to the principles that—
(a) every eligible elector should have a reasonable opportunity to vote;
(b) referendum processes should be conducted fairly, impartially, transparently, and efficiently;
(c) referendum results should accurately reflect the valid votes cast by electors; and
(d) public confidence in the integrity of referendum processes should be maintained.

SECTION: 5

HEADING: Act binds the Crown

This Act binds the Crown.

SECTION: 6

HEADING: Relationship with Electoral Act

1. The Electoral Act applies to a referendum except to the extent that this Act expressly provides otherwise.

2. If there is an inconsistency between this Act and the Electoral Act in relation to the conduct of a referendum, this Act prevails to the extent of the inconsistency.

PARTNUMBER: 2

PARTTITLE: Initiation of referendums

SECTION: 7

HEADING: Referendums may be initiated by Parliament

1. Parliament may provide by Act for a referendum to be held on any question that is not otherwise prohibited by law.

2. An Act providing for a referendum must specify—

(a) the referendum question; and

(b) whether the referendum is binding or indicative; and

(c) the date or period during which the referendum is to be held; and

(d) any special voting threshold required for the referendum; and

(e) any other matter necessary for the conduct of the referendum.

SECTION: 8

HEADING: Referendum initiated by resolution of Parliament

1. Parliament may, by resolution passed by a majority of the total number of Members of Parliament, direct that a referendum be held.

2. A resolution under subsection (1) must specify—

(a) the referendum question; and

(b) whether the referendum is binding or indicative; and

(c) the proposed polling date; and

(d) the voting threshold, if any.

3. A referendum directed by resolution under this section must be conducted in accordance with this Act.

SECTION: 9

HEADING: Referendum initiated by petition

1. A referendum may be initiated by a petition signed by not fewer than 10 percent of all enrolled electors.

2. A petition under subsection (1) must—

(a) state the referendum question; and

(b) state whether the proposed referendum is intended to be binding or indicative; and

(c) contain the full name and address of each person signing the petition; and

(d) contain the signature or other prescribed authentication of each person signing the petition; and

(e) comply with any other requirements prescribed by regulation.

SECTION: 10

HEADING: Form of referendum petition

1. A referendum petition must be in the prescribed form.

2. The Commission must publish a standard form for referendum petitions.

3. A petition may be submitted electronically if the Commission has approved an electronic method for submitting petitions.

SECTION: 11

HEADING: Petition promoters

1. Every referendum petition must have at least one promoter.

2. A promoter is responsible for—

(a) submitting the petition to the Commission;

(b) providing information reasonably required by the Commission;

(c) ensuring that the petition complies with this Act; and

(d) notifying the Commission of any material change affecting the petition.

SECTION: 12

HEADING: Verification of referendum petition

1. The Commission must verify every referendum petition submitted under [[section:9]].

2. The Commission may—

(a) compare signatures with electoral records;

(b) remove duplicate signatures;

(c) remove signatures of persons who are not eligible electors;

(d) remove signatures that cannot reasonably be authenticated; and

(e) require further information from the promoter.

3. The Commission must determine the number of valid signatures within 90 days after receiving the petition.

SECTION: 13

HEADING: Certification of petition

1. If the Commission is satisfied that a referendum petition contains the required number of valid signatures, it must certify the petition.

2. If the Commission determines that the petition does not contain the required number of valid signatures, it must reject the petition.

3. The Commission must publish its decision and the number of valid signatures counted.

SECTION: 14

HEADING: Effect of certified petition

1. A certified referendum petition must be referred to the House of Representatives.

2. The House must consider the petition within 60 sitting days after it is referred.

3. Parliament must, by Act or resolution, determine whether the referendum is to be held.

SECTION: 15

HEADING: Prohibited referendum subjects

A referendum must not be held on a question that—

(a) would require the Commission to act unlawfully; or

(b) concerns the appointment or removal of a judicial officer except where expressly authorised by law; or

(c) would determine the guilt or innocence of an individual; or

(d) concerns a matter that cannot lawfully be determined by referendum; or

(e) is otherwise prohibited by an Act of Parliament.

SECTION: 16

HEADING: Multiple referendum questions

1. More than one referendum may be held on the same polling day.

2. Each referendum question must be presented separately to electors.

3. The Commission must ensure that an elector can vote independently on each referendum question.

PARTNUMBER: 3

PARTTITLE: Referendum questions

SECTION: 17

HEADING: Requirements for referendum question

1. A referendum question must be expressed in clear and concise language.

2. A referendum question must be capable of being answered by selecting one of the options specified on the ballot paper.

3. A referendum question must not—

(a) contain language that is misleading or deceptive; or

(b) contain a statement that is presented as fact when the statement is disputed; or

(c) unfairly favour one possible answer.

SECTION: 18

HEADING: Commission approval of question

1. The Commission must review every proposed referendum question before the referendum is held.

2. The Commission may recommend amendments to the wording of a proposed question if the Commission considers that the question does not comply with [[section:17]].

3. The Commission must publish its assessment.

SECTION: 19

HEADING: Parliamentary approval of question

1. Where a referendum is established by Act of Parliament, the referendum question must be specified in that Act.

2. Where a referendum is initiated by petition, the Commission must submit the proposed question to Parliament for approval.

3. Parliament may amend the wording of the question if the amendment does not materially alter the subject of the proposed referendum.

SECTION: 20

HEADING: Two-option referendum

1. A referendum may provide electors with two possible answers.

2. Unless otherwise provided by the Act establishing the referendum, the ballot paper must contain the options—

(a) “Yes”; and

(b) “No”.

SECTION: 21

HEADING: Multiple-option referendum

1. A referendum may contain more than two possible answers if authorised by the Act establishing the referendum.

2. The Act must specify the method by which the winning option is to be determined.

SECTION: 22

HEADING: Approval of referendum question by elector

1. The Commission must publish an explanation of the referendum question before polling begins.

2. The explanation must state—

(a) the question to be put to electors;

(b) the effect of each possible answer;

(c) whether the referendum is binding or indicative; and

(d) any voting threshold that applies.

SECTION: 23

HEADING: Alteration of referendum question

1. A referendum question must not be altered after the official referendum period has begun.

2. If a referendum question is altered before the official referendum period begins, the Commission must publish the altered question and notify registered referendum campaigners.

PARTNUMBER: 4

PARTTITLE: Calling and administration of referendums

SECTION: 24

HEADING: Order for referendum

1. A referendum must be called by an order made by the Governor-General.

2. The order must specify—

(a) the referendum question;

(b) the polling day;

(c) whether the referendum is binding or indicative; and

(d) the voting threshold, if any.

SECTION: 25

HEADING: Polling day

1. The polling day for a referendum must be a Saturday unless Parliament otherwise provides.

2. Polling must be conducted during the hours prescribed by the Commission.

SECTION: 26

HEADING: Notice of referendum

1. The Commission must publish notice of a referendum at least 60 days before polling day.

2. The notice must include—

(a) the referendum question;

(b) the polling day;

(c) the voting system;

(d) the applicable voting threshold;

(e) information about advance and special voting; and

(f) information about referendum campaign finance.

SECTION: 27

HEADING: Referendum timetable

The Commission must publish a timetable specifying the deadlines for—

(a) registration of campaigners;

(b) applications for special voting;

(c) submission of campaign finance returns;

(d) appointment of scrutineers;

(e) advance voting; and

(f) any other matter necessary for the administration of the referendum.

SECTION: 28

HEADING: Returning officers

1. The Commission must appoint a returning officer for each electoral district in which a referendum is held.

2. A returning officer is responsible for—

(a) establishing polling places;

(b) supervising polling;

(c) securing ballot papers;

(d) counting votes; and

(e) reporting the preliminary result to the Commission.

SECTION: 29

HEADING: Polling places

1. The Commission must establish sufficient polling places to enable electors to vote conveniently.

2. The Commission must have regard to—

(a) population;

(b) geographic accessibility;

(c) accessibility for persons with disabilities; and

(d) the expected number of electors.

SECTION: 30

HEADING: Ballot papers

1. The Commission must prepare ballot papers for every referendum.

2. A ballot paper must—

(a) contain the referendum question;

(b) contain the voting options;

(c) provide clear instructions for voting; and

(d) contain any security features prescribed by the Commission.

SECTION: 31

HEADING: Security of ballot papers

1. The Commission must take reasonable steps to prevent—

(a) unauthorised access to ballot papers;

(b) theft or destruction of ballot papers;

(c) fraudulent reproduction of ballot papers; and

(d) interference with the counting of votes.

2. A person who knowingly interferes with ballot papers commits an offence.

PARTNUMBER: 5

PARTTITLE: Entitlement to vote

SECTION: 32

HEADING: Entitlement to vote

1. A person is entitled to vote in a referendum if the person—

(a) is enrolled as an elector; and

(b) is otherwise entitled to vote under the Electoral Act.

2. A person may vote only once in each referendum.

SECTION: 33

HEADING: Electoral roll for referendum

1. The electoral roll used for a referendum must be the roll prepared under the Electoral Act.

2. The Commission may make administrative amendments to the roll for the purpose of conducting the referendum.

SECTION: 34

HEADING: Voting in referendum

1. An elector must indicate their preferred answer in the manner specified on the ballot paper.

2. A ballot paper is informal if—

(a) the elector cannot reasonably be identified as having selected an answer; or

(b) the ballot paper contains information that identifies the elector; or

(c) the ballot paper is otherwise invalid under regulations made under this Act.

SECTION: 35

HEADING: Assistance to voters

1. An elector who requires assistance to vote may receive assistance in accordance with the Electoral Act.

2. A person assisting an elector must not influence the elector's vote.

SECTION: 36

HEADING: Advance voting

1. Advance voting may be conducted during the period specified by the Commission.

2. An elector voting in advance must comply with the same voting requirements as an elector voting on polling day.

SECTION: 37

HEADING: Special voting

1. An elector may cast a special vote if the elector is unable to vote at an ordinary polling place for a reason authorised under the Electoral Act.

2. Special votes must be processed and counted in accordance with regulations made under this Act.

SECTION: 38

HEADING: Overseas voting

1. An elector who is overseas may vote in a referendum if entitled to vote under the Electoral Act.

2. The Commission may establish facilities for overseas voting.

SECTION: 39

HEADING: Secrecy of referendum vote

1. Every elector is entitled to vote in secret.

2. A person must not—

(a) require an elector to disclose how the elector voted;

(b) observe an elector marking a ballot paper;

(c) interfere with an elector while voting; or

(d) obtain or attempt to obtain information about how another person voted.

3. A person who contravenes this section commits an offence.

PARTNUMBER: 6

PARTTITLE: Referendum campaigners

SECTION: 40

HEADING: Registration of referendum campaigners

1. A person or organisation must register with the Commission before conducting a referendum campaign if the person or organisation—

(a) incurs referendum expenditure above the prescribed threshold; or

(b) receives referendum donations above the prescribed threshold.

2. The Commission must maintain a register of referendum campaigners.

SECTION: 41

HEADING: Application for registration

An application for registration as a referendum campaigner must—

(a) be made in the prescribed form;

(b) identify the campaigner;

(c) identify whether the campaigner supports or opposes the referendum proposal;

(d) state the name of the financial agent; and

(e) contain any other information required by the Commission.

SECTION: 42

HEADING: Financial agent

1. Every registered referendum campaigner must appoint a financial agent.

2. The financial agent is responsible for—

(a) authorising referendum expenditure;

(b) receiving referendum donations;

(c) maintaining financial records;

(d) filing expenditure returns; and

(e) ensuring compliance with this Act.

SECTION: 43

HEADING: Campaigner names

1. A referendum campaigner must not use a name that—

(a) is misleading or deceptive;

(b) falsely suggests that the campaigner represents the Government or Commission; or

(c) is substantially similar to the name of another registered campaigner.

SECTION: 44

HEADING: Deregistration of campaigners

1. A campaigner may apply to cancel its registration.

2. The Commission may cancel a campaigner's registration if—

(a) the campaigner ceases to exist;

(b) the campaigner obtained registration by fraud;

(c) the campaigner repeatedly breaches this Act; or

(d) the campaigner is otherwise no longer eligible for registration.

3. Before cancelling registration, the Commission must—

(a) notify the campaigner;

(b) invite submissions; and

(c) consider any submissions received.

PARTNUMBER: 7

PARTTITLE: Referendum advertising

SECTION: 45

HEADING: Referendum advertisements

1. A referendum advertisement is an advertisement that may reasonably be regarded as encouraging voters to vote for or against a referendum proposal.

2. A referendum advertisement must comply with this Part.

SECTION: 46

HEADING: Authorisation statement

1. Every referendum advertisement must contain an authorisation statement.

2. The authorisation statement must—

(a) identify the person or organisation responsible for the advertisement; and

(b) state the physical or electronic address of that person or organisation.

3. The Commission may prescribe the form of an authorisation statement.

SECTION: 47

HEADING: False or misleading referendum advertising

A person must not publish or distribute a referendum advertisement that—

(a) contains a statement that the person knows to be materially false; or

(b) deliberately misrepresents the effect of the referendum proposal; or

(c) falsely claims that the advertisement has been authorised by the Commission.

SECTION: 48

HEADING: Government referendum advertising

1. The Government may publish factual information concerning a referendum.

2. Government information must—

(a) be accurate;

(b) be impartial;

(c) explain the legal and practical effect of the proposal; and

(d) not expressly encourage electors to vote for or against the proposal.

SECTION: 49

HEADING: Referendum information booklet

1. The Commission must prepare an official referendum information booklet.

2. The booklet must contain—

(a) the referendum question;

(b) an impartial explanation of the proposal;

(c) arguments supporting the proposal;

(d) arguments opposing the proposal; and

(e) information about voting.

3. The Commission must take reasonable steps to ensure that the booklet is distributed to every household.

SECTION: 50

HEADING: Arguments for and against

1. The Commission must invite registered campaigners supporting and opposing the referendum proposal to submit arguments for publication in the official information booklet.

2. The Commission may impose a reasonable word limit.

3. The Commission must not alter the substance of an argument except to correct—

(a) spelling;

(b) grammar;

(c) formatting; or

(d) material that is unlawful.

SECTION: 51

HEADING: Digital referendum advertising

1. A digital referendum advertisement is subject to the same requirements as any other referendum advertisement.

2. A digital advertisement must contain an authorisation statement that is reasonably visible to the viewer.

3. A person must not deliberately conceal the identity of the person responsible for a referendum advertisement.

SECTION: 52

HEADING: Third-party campaigners

1. A person who is not a registered referendum campaigner may publish referendum material provided that—

(a) the person complies with the authorisation requirements; and

(b) the person's expenditure does not exceed the prescribed threshold.

2. A person who exceeds the threshold must register as a referendum campaigner.

PARTNUMBER: 8

PARTTITLE: Referendum finance

SECTION: 53

HEADING: Referendum expenditure

Referendum expenditure includes expenditure incurred for the purpose of—

(a) promoting a vote for a referendum proposal;

(b) promoting a vote against a referendum proposal;

(c) encouraging participation in a referendum; or

(d) otherwise influencing the outcome of a referendum.

SECTION: 54

HEADING: Spending limits

1. The maximum amount that a registered referendum campaigner may spend during a referendum campaign is ₴1,500,000.

2. A campaigner must not knowingly avoid the limit by dividing expenditure between associated persons or organisations.

3. The Commission may prescribe lower limits for local referendums.

SECTION: 55

HEADING: Donations

1. A referendum campaigner may receive donations for referendum purposes.

2. A donation must not be accepted if the campaigner knows that the donor is prohibited from making the donation.

3. Donations must be recorded in accordance with this Act.

SECTION: 56

HEADING: Anonymous donations

1. A campaigner must not accept an anonymous donation exceeding ₴1,000.

2. An anonymous donation exceeding that amount must be returned to the donor if the donor can be identified.

3. If the donor cannot be identified, the donation must be paid to the Commission.

SECTION: 57

HEADING: Disclosure of large donations

1. A referendum campaigner must disclose every donation exceeding ₴10,000.

2. The disclosure must state—

(a) the amount of the donation;

(b) the date on which it was received; and

(c) the identity of the donor.

SECTION: 58

HEADING: Financial records

1. Every registered referendum campaigner must keep complete and accurate financial records.

2. Records must be retained for at least 7 years after polling day.

SECTION: 59

HEADING: Return of referendum expenditure

1. Every registered referendum campaigner must submit a return of referendum expenditure to the Commission.

2. The return must include—

(a) total expenditure;

(b) expenditure by category;

(c) the identity of suppliers receiving more than ₴5,000;

(d) donations received; and

(e) any other information prescribed by regulation.

SECTION: 60

HEADING: Time for filing return

A referendum expenditure return must be filed within 90 days after polling day.

SECTION: 61

HEADING: Audit of campaign returns

1. The Commission may audit a referendum campaigner's financial return.

2. A campaigner must provide documents reasonably required for an audit.

3. A person who knowingly provides false information during an audit commits an offence.

PARTNUMBER: 9

PARTTITLE: Polling and counting

SECTION: 62

HEADING: Conduct of polling

1. Polling must be conducted in accordance with this Act and the Electoral Act.

2. The Commission must take reasonable steps to ensure that—

(a) polling is conducted fairly;

(b) ballot papers are securely handled;

(c) electors are able to vote without intimidation; and

(d) the secrecy of voting is maintained.

SECTION: 63

HEADING: Close of poll

1. Polling closes at the time specified by the Commission.

2. A person who is in a polling place at the time polling closes may vote if otherwise entitled to do so.

SECTION: 64

HEADING: Preliminary count

1. As soon as practicable after polling closes, the returning officer must conduct a preliminary count.

2. The preliminary count must be conducted in the presence of authorised observers.

3. The preliminary result must be transmitted to the Commission.

SECTION: 65

HEADING: Informal votes

1. A ballot paper is informal if it does not clearly indicate the elector's choice.

2. The returning officer must determine whether a ballot paper is informal.

3. A ballot paper must not be rejected merely because of a minor error if the elector's intention is clear.

SECTION: 66

HEADING: Special vote count

1. Special votes must be counted after the close of ordinary polling.

2. A special vote may be counted only if the Commission is satisfied that the elector was entitled to vote.

SECTION: 67

HEADING: Official count

1. The Commission must conduct an official count after polling day.

2. The official count must include—

(a) ordinary votes;

(b) advance votes;

(c) special votes; and

(d) overseas votes.

SECTION: 68

HEADING: Recount

1. The Commission may order a recount if—

(a) the margin between the leading options is sufficiently small to justify a recount; or

(b) a material counting error is suspected; or

(c) otherwise considers a recount necessary.

2. A recount must be conducted in accordance with procedures prescribed by the Commission.

SECTION: 69

HEADING: Observers

1. A registered referendum campaigner may appoint observers to observe the counting of votes.

2. An observer must not interfere with the counting process.

3. The Commission may remove an observer who breaches this section.

PARTNUMBER: 10

PARTTITLE: Determination of result

SECTION: 70

HEADING: Declaration of result

1. The Commission must declare the result of a referendum as soon as practicable after the official count is completed.

2. The declaration must state—

(a) the number of votes cast for each option;

(b) the number of informal votes;

(c) the total number of votes cast; and

(d) whether the proposal has passed or failed.

SECTION: 71

HEADING: Simple majority

1. Unless another threshold is specified by law, a referendum proposal is approved if more valid votes are cast in favour of the proposal than against it.

2. If more valid votes are cast against the proposal than in favour, the proposal is rejected.

SECTION: 72

HEADING: Special majority

1. An Act establishing a referendum may require a special majority for approval.

2. A special majority may include—

(a) a specified percentage of valid votes;

(b) a specified proportion of enrolled electors; or

(c) approval in a specified number of electoral districts.

SECTION: 73

HEADING: Binding referendum

1. A referendum is binding only if the Act or resolution establishing the referendum expressly provides that it is binding.

2. If a binding referendum approves a proposal, the Government must give effect to the proposal in accordance with the Act establishing the referendum.

SECTION: 74

HEADING: Indicative referendum

1. An indicative referendum expresses the view of electors but does not itself alter the law.

2. The Government must publish a response to an indicative referendum within 90 days after the declaration of the result.

SECTION: 75

HEADING: Publication of result

The Commission must publish the official result—

(a) on its website;

(b) in the Gazette; and

(c) in any other manner the Commission considers appropriate.

PARTNUMBER: 11

PARTTITLE: Effect of referendum

SECTION: 76

HEADING: Effect of approval

1. If a binding referendum approves a proposal to enact, amend, or repeal legislation, the relevant legislation must be enacted, amended, or repealed in accordance with the referendum Act.

2. A referendum does not itself amend an Act unless the referendum Act expressly provides that it does so.

SECTION: 77

HEADING: Effect of rejection

1. If a referendum proposal is rejected, the proposal has no legal effect.

2. The rejection of a proposal does not prevent Parliament from considering a substantially similar proposal unless the referendum Act provides otherwise.

SECTION: 78

HEADING: Repetition of referendum

1. A referendum on substantially the same question must not be held within 3 years after the previous referendum unless—

(a) Parliament resolves otherwise by a majority of the total number of Members of Parliament; or

(b) the law has materially changed since the previous referendum.

SECTION: 79

HEADING: No effect on existing rights

Unless expressly provided otherwise, a referendum does not—

(a) retrospectively invalidate an act done before the referendum;

(b) affect a person's existing legal proceedings; or

(c) remove a right or obligation that arose before the referendum.

PARTNUMBER: 12

PARTTITLE: Challenges and judicial review

SECTION: 80

HEADING: Challenge to conduct of referendum

1. A person may apply to the High Court for a declaration that a referendum was not conducted in accordance with this Act.

2. An application must be made within 28 days after the declaration of the result.

SECTION: 81

HEADING: Grounds for challenge

A referendum may be challenged on the ground that—

(a) a material irregularity occurred in the conduct of polling;

(b) votes were improperly counted;

(c) unlawful interference materially affected the result;

(d) fraudulent voting materially affected the result; or

(e) the Commission acted unlawfully in determining the result.

SECTION: 82

HEADING: Effect of irregularity

The High Court must not invalidate a referendum merely because of an irregularity unless satisfied that the irregularity could reasonably have affected the result.

SECTION: 83

HEADING: Order for fresh referendum

1. If the High Court is satisfied that the result of a referendum was materially affected by an irregularity, it may order that a fresh referendum be held.

2. A fresh referendum must be conducted in accordance with this Act.

SECTION: 84

HEADING: Commission may correct errors

1. The Commission may correct an administrative or clerical error in a referendum result.

2. The Commission must publish notice of any correction that materially affects the result.

SECTION: 85

HEADING: Finality of result

Subject to [[section:80]], the result declared by the Commission is final and conclusive.

PARTNUMBER: 13

PARTTITLE: Offences

SECTION: 86

HEADING: False voting

A person commits an offence if the person knowingly—

(a) votes more than once in a referendum;

(b) votes in the name of another person; or

(c) votes knowing that the person is not entitled to vote.

SECTION: 87

HEADING: Interference with voting

A person commits an offence if the person—

(a) intimidates an elector;

(b) prevents an elector from voting;

(c) improperly influences an elector at a polling place; or

(d) interferes with the secrecy of an elector's vote.

SECTION: 88

HEADING: Bribery

A person commits an offence if the person—

(a) gives or offers a benefit to another person to influence how that person votes; or

(b) receives or agrees to receive a benefit in return for voting in a particular manner.

SECTION: 89

HEADING: Fraudulent referendum material

A person commits an offence if the person knowingly publishes referendum material that—

(a) falsely claims to be issued by the Commission;

(b) falsely claims to be official referendum material; or

(c) materially misrepresents the referendum question.

SECTION: 90

HEADING: Unauthorised campaign expenditure

A person commits an offence if the person knowingly incurs referendum expenditure in contravention of a spending limit imposed by this Act.

SECTION: 91

HEADING: False financial return

A person commits an offence if the person knowingly submits a referendum financial return containing materially false or misleading information.

SECTION: 92

HEADING: Penalties

1. A person convicted of an offence under this Part is liable—

(a) in the case of an individual, to a fine not exceeding ₴50,000; or

(b) in the case of a body corporate, to a fine not exceeding ₴250,000.

2. A court may impose a higher penalty where another Act provides for a higher penalty for the same conduct.

PARTNUMBER: 14

PARTTITLE: Enforcement and compliance

SECTION: 93

HEADING: Compliance notices

1. The Commission may issue a compliance notice to a person whom it reasonably believes is contravening this Act.

2. A compliance notice may require the person to—

(a) stop publishing specified material;

(b) correct a statement;

(c) provide information;

(d) amend a financial return; or

(e) take any other reasonable step to remedy the contravention.

SECTION: 94

HEADING: Commission investigations

1. The Commission may investigate an alleged contravention of this Act.

2. For the purposes of an investigation, the Commission may require a person to provide documents or information reasonably necessary for the investigation.

SECTION: 95

HEADING: Powers of entry

1. An authorised officer may enter premises used for referendum campaign purposes if—

(a) the occupier consents; or

(b) a warrant has been issued by a court.

2. An authorised officer exercising powers under this section must produce evidence of their authority on request.

SECTION: 96

HEADING: Protection from liability

The Commission, a member of the Commission, and an authorised officer are not personally liable for an act or omission done in good faith in the exercise of powers under this Act.

PARTNUMBER: 15

PARTTITLE: Regulations and miscellaneous provisions

SECTION: 97

HEADING: Regulations

The Governor-General may, by Order in Council, make regulations for the purpose of—

(a) prescribing forms;

(b) prescribing procedures for referendum petitions;

(c) prescribing campaign expenditure limits;

(d) prescribing financial disclosure requirements;

(e) prescribing procedures for counting votes;

(f) prescribing requirements for electronic voting or electronic petitions; and

(g) prescribing any other matter necessary for carrying out or giving effect to this Act.

SECTION: 98

HEADING: Commission guidelines

1. The Commission may issue guidelines concerning the administration of referendums.

2. Guidelines issued under this section must be published.

3. Guidelines do not have the force of law unless expressly provided by an Act or regulation.

SECTION: 99

HEADING: Annual report

The Commission must include in its annual report information about—

(a) referendums conducted during the year;

(b) expenditure incurred by the Commission;

(c) campaign finance returns;

(d) offences and enforcement action; and

(e) any recommendations for improving the administration of referendums.

SECTION: 100

HEADING: Review of Act

1. The Minister must arrange for this Act to be reviewed within 5 years after its commencement.

2. The review must consider—

(a) the effectiveness of referendum procedures;

(b) campaign finance rules;

(c) the regulation of referendum advertising;

(d) public participation;

(e) accessibility of voting; and

(f) any other matter relevant to the operation of this Act.

3. A report of the review must be presented to Parliament.

SECTION: 101

HEADING: Transitional provision

1. A referendum initiated before the commencement of this Act may be conducted under the law in force immediately before commencement.

2. The Commission may apply provisions of this Act to a referendum referred to in subsection (1) where necessary for administrative consistency.

SECTION: 102

HEADING: Consequential amendments

The enactments specified in Schedule 1 are amended in accordance with that Schedule.

SECTION: 103

HEADING: Repeals

The enactments specified in Schedule 2 are repealed to the extent specified in that Schedule.

SECTION: 104

HEADING: Saving provision

Nothing in this Act affects—

(a) anything lawfully done under an enactment repealed or amended by this Act;

(b) a right or obligation that arose before the commencement of this Act; or

(c) any proceeding commenced before the commencement of this Act.

PARTNUMBER: 16

PARTTITLE: Referendum integrity

SECTION: 105

HEADING: Duty to maintain integrity of referendum

1. Every person exercising a function under this Act must act so as to maintain the integrity of the referendum.

2. The Commission must take reasonable steps to protect the referendum from—

(a) fraud;

(b) intimidation;

(c) unlawful foreign interference;

(d) unauthorised manipulation of voting systems;

(e) unlawful campaign finance; and

(f) other conduct capable of materially undermining public confidence in the referendum.

SECTION: 106

HEADING: Foreign interference

1. A person must not knowingly accept referendum campaign funding from a foreign government.

2. A person must not knowingly use referendum campaign funding for the purpose of concealing foreign government involvement in a referendum campaign.

3. A person who contravenes this section commits an offence.

SECTION: 107

HEADING: Misuse of Commission name

A person must not represent that referendum campaign material—

(a) has been approved by the Commission; or

(b) is issued by the Commission;

unless the representation is authorised by the Commission.

SECTION: 108

HEADING: Protection of referendum systems

1. A person must not knowingly interfere with an electronic system used by the Commission for the administration of a referendum.

2. A person must not—

(a) alter referendum results without authority;

(b) destroy referendum records;

(c) gain unauthorised access to referendum systems; or

(d) knowingly introduce malicious code into a referendum system.

3. A person who contravenes this section commits an offence.

PARTNUMBER: 17

PARTTITLE: Final provisions

SECTION: 109

HEADING: Delegation by Commission

1. The Commission may delegate any of its functions under this Act to—

(a) a returning officer;

(b) an employee of the Commission; or

(c) another person authorised by the Commission.

2. The Commission must not delegate the power to—

(a) declare the final result of a referendum;

(b) certify a referendum petition; or

(c) issue regulations.

SECTION: 110

HEADING: Records of referendum

1. The Commission must retain referendum records for at least 10 years after polling day.

2. Records must include—

(a) ballot papers;

(b) counting records;

(c) campaign finance returns;

(d) referendum petitions;

(e) official results; and

(f) other records prescribed by regulation.

SECTION: 111

HEADING: Public access to referendum information

1. The Commission must make publicly available, as soon as reasonably practicable—

(a) the referendum question;

(b) the official information booklet;

(c) registered campaigners;

(d) campaign finance returns;

(e) the official result; and

(f) any report concerning the administration of the referendum.

SECTION: 112

HEADING: Protection of personal information

1. The Commission must not publish personal information collected under this Act unless publication is authorised by this Act or another enactment.

2. The Commission must take reasonable steps to protect personal information from unauthorised disclosure.

SECTION: 113

HEADING: Electronic administration

1. The Commission may provide for any function under this Act to be performed electronically.

2. Electronic systems used under this section must provide reasonable security, reliability, accessibility, and auditability.

SECTION: 114

HEADING: Crown expenditure

All expenditure incurred by the Crown in giving effect to a referendum authorised under this Act is payable out of money appropriated by Parliament.

SECTION: 115

HEADING: Review of referendum result

Nothing in this Act prevents Parliament from reviewing the political or policy consequences of a referendum result.

SECTION: 116

HEADING: Act to be read as a whole

This Act must be read as a whole to give effect to its purpose of ensuring that referendums in Square Country are conducted freely, fairly, transparently, and with public confidence in their integrity.
