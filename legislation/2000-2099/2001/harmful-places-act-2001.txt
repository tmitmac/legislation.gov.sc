TITLE: Harmful Places Act 2001

ACTNUMBER: 2001 No 47

TYPE: Act

STATUS: In force

VERSION: Latest

VERSIONTITLE: as at 22 September 2026

AGENCY: the Ministry of Environment and Local Government

DATEOFASSENT: 18 December 2001

COMMENCEMENT: see section 2

PARTNUMBER: 1

PARTTITLE: Preliminary Provisions

SECTION: 1

HEADING: Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Harmful Places Act 2001**.

SECTION: 2

HEADING: Commencement

1. This Act comes into force on 1 March 2002.
2. Different provisions of this Act may be brought into force on different dates by Order in Council.

SECTION: 3

HEADING: Interpretation

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires—

**authorised officer** means a person appointed as an authorised officer under section 35.

**dangerous place** means any place which presents, or is likely to present, a serious and immediate risk to the life, health, or safety of persons entering or remaining in that place.

**harmful place** means any place which, by reason of its condition, use, activities, substances, structures, environmental conditions, or other circumstances, presents or is likely to present a material risk of harm to persons or property.

**occupier**, in relation to any place, means the person who has the right to occupy that place or, where there is no such person, the person apparently in charge of that place.

**owner** means, in relation to any place, the person who holds the legal estate or other legal interest in that place.

**place** includes any land, building, structure, road, reserve, beach, river, lake, waterway, foreshore, or other area, whether public or private.

**regional council** means a regional council established under the Local Government Act 1989.

**restriction notice** means a notice issued under section 18.

**serious risk** means a risk which is reasonably capable of causing death, serious injury, significant illness, or substantial damage.

SECTION: 4

HEADING: Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to provide for the identification and control of places which are harmful or dangerous to persons or property, and in particular—

(a) to provide for the declaration of harmful and dangerous places; and

(b) to confer powers upon regional councils to restrict or prohibit access to such places; and

(c) to provide for the inspection and supervision of such places; and

(d) to provide for the taking of measures to remove or reduce hazards; and

(e) to provide for the protection of members of the public from unnecessary exposure to danger.

SECTION: 5

HEADING: Act binds the Crown

This Act binds the Crown.

SECTION: 6

HEADING: Relationship with other enactments

1. Nothing in this Act limits any power conferred by any other enactment in relation to public safety, environmental protection, emergency management, building safety, or public health.
2. A power exercised under this Act shall, so far as practicable, be exercised consistently with other enactments.

AMENDMENT: Section 6(2): inserted, on 14 October 2011, by section 4 of the Harmful Places Amendment Act 2011 (2011 No 31).

PARTNUMBER: 2

PARTTITLE: Identification and Declaration of Places

SECTION: 7

HEADING: Functions of regional councils

1. Every regional council shall take such steps as are reasonably practicable to identify places within its region which may constitute harmful places or dangerous places.
2. In exercising its functions under this Act, a regional council shall have regard to—
   (a) the nature of the hazard;
   (b) the seriousness of the hazard;
   (c) the likelihood of persons being exposed to the hazard;
   (d) the number of persons likely to be affected; and
   (e) the availability of reasonable measures for reducing the risk.

SECTION: 8

HEADING: Investigation of places

1. A regional council may investigate any place where it has reasonable grounds to believe that the place may be a harmful place or dangerous place.
2. For the purposes of subsection (1), the council may—
   (a) inspect the place;
   (b) inspect any building, structure, machinery, equipment, or substance;
   (c) take photographs, measurements, or samples;
   (d) require information from the owner or occupier; and
   (e) obtain expert advice.
3. The council shall, where practicable, give reasonable notice of an inspection to the owner or occupier.

SECTION: 9

HEADING: Notice requiring information

1. A regional council may require the owner or occupier of a place to provide information relating to any hazard existing at that place.
2. A notice under this section may require information relating to—
   (a) the nature of the hazard;
   (b) the activities undertaken at the place;
   (c) measures taken to control the hazard; and
   (d) the persons who may reasonably be expected to be exposed to the hazard.
3. A person upon whom a notice is served shall comply with the notice within the time specified therein.

SECTION: 10

HEADING: Declaration of harmful place

1. Where a regional council is satisfied that a place presents a material risk of harm, the council may, by notice, declare that place to be a harmful place.
2. A declaration under subsection (1) shall specify—
   (a) the place concerned;
   (b) the nature of the hazard;
   (c) the reasons for the declaration; and
   (d) any restriction or requirement imposed by the council.

SECTION: 11

HEADING: Declaration of dangerous place

1. Where a regional council is satisfied that a place presents a serious and immediate risk to persons, the council may declare the place to be a dangerous place.
2. A declaration under this section may be made notwithstanding that the place has not previously been declared a harmful place.
3. The declaration shall remain in force until revoked under this Act.

SECTION: 12

HEADING: Matters to be considered before declaration

Before declaring any place under section 10 or section 11, the regional council shall, so far as practicable, consider—

(a) whether the danger may be removed or reduced;

(b) whether warning signs would be sufficient;

(c) whether access may be restricted rather than prohibited;

(d) the interests of the owner and occupier; and

(e) the interests of persons who ordinarily use the place.

SECTION: 12A

HEADING: Certain places deemed harmful places

1. The Governor-General may, by Order in Council, declare a class of places to be harmful places for the purposes of this Act.
2. No declaration under this section shall prevent a regional council from determining that a particular place within that class is not a harmful place where the council is satisfied that the relevant risk does not exist.

AMENDMENT: Section 12A: inserted, on 22 June 2006, by section 5 of the Harmful Places Amendment Act 2006 (2006 No 18).

SECTION: 13

HEADING: Notice of declaration

1. A regional council declaring a place under section 10 or section 11 shall cause notice of the declaration to be given to—
   (a) the owner;
   (b) the occupier, if any; and
   (c) such other persons as the council considers likely to be materially affected.
2. The council shall also cause reasonable public notice of the declaration to be given.

SECTION: 14

HEADING: Register of harmful places

1. Every regional council shall maintain a register of places declared under this Act.
2. The register shall contain—
   (a) a description of the place;
   (b) the date of declaration;
   (c) the nature of the hazard;
   (d) the restrictions applying to the place; and
   (e) the date of the most recent review.
3. The register shall be available for inspection by members of the public.

AMENDMENT: Section 14(3): amended, on 3 March 2018, by section 7 of the Harmful Places Amendment Act 2018 (2018 No 09).

PARTNUMBER: 3

PARTTITLE: Restrictions on Access

SECTION: 15

HEADING: Power to restrict access

1. A regional council may restrict access to any harmful place where it considers such restriction necessary for the protection of persons.
2. A restriction may—
   (a) prohibit entry;
   (b) prohibit entry during specified times;
   (c) prohibit particular activities;
   (d) restrict entry to particular persons;
   (e) require supervision; or
   (f) apply to any specified part of the place.

SECTION: 16

HEADING: Closure of dangerous places

1. Where a place has been declared a dangerous place, the regional council may order that the place be closed.
2. An order under subsection (1) may prohibit all persons from entering the place except persons authorised under this Act.
3. The council may maintain the closure for so long as it considers necessary.

SECTION: 17

HEADING: Conditional access

1. A regional council may permit access to a restricted place subject to such conditions as it thinks fit.
2. Without limiting subsection (1), conditions may relate to—
   (a) protective clothing or equipment;
   (b) supervision;
   (c) the time of entry;
   (d) the number of persons entering;
   (e) the route to be followed; or
   (f) any other reasonable safety requirement.

SECTION: 18

HEADING: Restriction notices

1. A restriction notice shall state—
   (a) the place to which it applies;
   (b) the nature of the restriction;
   (c) the reason for imposing the restriction; and
   (d) the date upon which the restriction takes effect.
2. A restriction notice may be amended or revoked by the regional council.

SECTION: 18A

HEADING: Electronic publication of notices

1. A regional council may publish any notice under this Act by electronic means in addition to any other form of publication required by this Act.
2. Publication under this section does not remove any requirement to give notice directly to an owner or occupier.

AMENDMENT: Section 18A: inserted, on 17 September 2015, by section 8 of the Harmful Places Amendment Act 2015 (2015 No 42).

SECTION: 19

HEADING: Warning signs

1. A regional council may erect warning signs at or near any harmful place or dangerous place.
2. A warning sign may state—
   (a) that the place is dangerous or harmful;
   (b) that entry is prohibited or restricted;
   (c) the nature of the hazard; and
   (d) any conditions applying to entry.
3. No person shall intentionally remove, damage, obscure, or deface a warning sign erected under this Act.

SECTION: 20

HEADING: Fences and barriers

1. A regional council may erect fences, gates, barriers, or other structures for the purpose of preventing or controlling access to a place.
2. The owner or occupier may be required to maintain such measures where specified by notice.
3. No person shall intentionally interfere with any fence, gate, or barrier established under this Act.

SECTION: 21

HEADING: Exceptions for authorised persons

Nothing in this Part prevents entry by—

(a) a member of the police;

(b) a firefighter;

(c) an ambulance officer;

(d) an authorised officer;

(e) a person carrying out emergency work; or

(f) any other person authorised by the regional council.

SECTION: 22

HEADING: Public rights and existing interests

A restriction imposed under this Part shall not, by itself, extinguish any right of ownership, easement, right of way, or other lawful interest in land.

PARTNUMBER: 4

PARTTITLE: Emergency Powers

SECTION: 23

HEADING: Immediate closure

1. Where an authorised officer is satisfied that an immediate danger exists, the officer may close a place or prohibit access to it without first obtaining the approval of the regional council.
2. An officer exercising the power under subsection (1) shall report the action to the regional council as soon as practicable.
3. A closure under this section shall expire 72 hours after it is imposed unless continued by the regional council.

SECTION: 23A

HEADING: Emergency warning

1. Where the circumstances require immediate action, an authorised officer may cause a warning to be issued by any reasonable means.
2. A warning under this section may include a direction to leave the place immediately.

AMENDMENT: Section 23A: inserted, on 8 May 2009, by section 9 of the Harmful Places Amendment Act 2009 (2009 No 22).

SECTION: 24

HEADING: Directions to leave

1. An authorised officer may direct any person to leave a harmful place or dangerous place.
2. A person who is directed to leave shall leave the place as soon as reasonably practicable.
3. This section does not apply where compliance would place the person in greater danger.

SECTION: 25

HEADING: Evacuation

1. A regional council may direct the evacuation of a place where it considers that persons are exposed to serious danger.
2. An evacuation direction may apply to the whole or any part of a place.
3. Every person to whom an evacuation direction applies shall comply with it.

SECTION: 26

HEADING: Emergency services

Nothing in this Act limits the powers of an emergency service to enter a place where entry is necessary to save life or protect property.

PARTNUMBER: 5

PARTTITLE: Duties of Owners and Occupiers

SECTION: 27

HEADING: Duty to notify council of serious hazards

1. Every owner or occupier who becomes aware of a serious hazard at a place shall, as soon as practicable, notify the regional council.
2. The notification shall contain such particulars of the hazard as are reasonably known to the owner or occupier.

SECTION: 28

HEADING: Duty to take precautions

1. The owner or occupier of a harmful place shall take all reasonable precautions to prevent unnecessary exposure of persons to any known hazard.
2. Without limiting subsection (1), the owner or occupier may be required to—
   (a) secure the place;
   (b) remove the hazard;
   (c) provide warnings;
   (d) restrict access; or
   (e) undertake remedial work.

SECTION: 29

HEADING: Remedial notice

1. A regional council may serve a notice requiring an owner or occupier to carry out specified work.
2. The notice shall specify the work required and the period within which it is to be completed.
3. The council shall allow a reasonable period for compliance except where the hazard is immediate.

SECTION: 30

HEADING: Failure to carry out remedial work

1. Where an owner or occupier fails to comply with a remedial notice, the regional council may carry out the work itself.
2. The reasonable cost of work undertaken under this section may be recovered from the owner or occupier as a debt due to the council.

SECTION: 31

HEADING: Emergency remedial work

Where the regional council considers that immediate work is necessary to prevent serious harm, it may undertake or arrange for such work to be undertaken without first serving a remedial notice.

SECTION: 32

HEADING: Recovery of costs

The costs incurred by a regional council under section 30 or section 31 may be recovered in a court of competent jurisdiction.

PARTNUMBER: 6

PARTTITLE: Inspection and Enforcement

SECTION: 33

HEADING: Power of entry

1. An authorised officer may enter a place at any reasonable time for the purpose of determining whether this Act is being complied with.
2. An authorised officer may enter without notice where the officer has reasonable grounds to believe that immediate entry is necessary to prevent serious harm.

SECTION: 34

HEADING: Powers of inspection

An authorised officer entering a place under this Act may—

(a) inspect the place;

(b) inspect any structure, equipment, machinery, or substance;

(c) take photographs or measurements;

(d) take samples;

(e) require reasonable information; and

(f) undertake any other reasonable inspection necessary for the purposes of this Act.

SECTION: 35

HEADING: Appointment of authorised officers

1. A regional council may appoint persons to be authorised officers for the purposes of this Act.
2. An authorised officer shall carry evidence of appointment and shall produce it on request.

SECTION: 35A

HEADING: Identification cards

1. A regional council shall issue an identification card to every authorised officer.
2. The card shall contain—
   (a) the name of the officer;
   (b) a photograph of the officer;
   (c) the date of appointment; and
   (d) such other particulars as may be prescribed.
3. An authorised officer exercising a power under this Act shall, on request, produce the identification card.

AMENDMENT: Section 35A: inserted, on 12 February 2004, by section 6 of the Harmful Places Amendment Act 2004 (2004 No 06).

SECTION: 36

HEADING: Obstruction of authorised officers

No person shall obstruct, hinder, threaten, or deceive an authorised officer exercising a power under this Act.

SECTION: 37

HEADING: Warrants

1. A District Court Judge may issue a warrant authorising an authorised officer to enter a place where satisfied that there are reasonable grounds to believe that evidence of an offence against this Act is present.
2. A warrant may authorise the use of reasonable force.

PARTNUMBER: 7

PARTTITLE: Review of Declarations and Restrictions

SECTION: 38

HEADING: Review by regional council

1. An owner or occupier affected by a declaration or restriction may apply to the regional council for a review.
2. An application shall be made in writing and shall state the grounds upon which the review is sought.
3. The council shall consider the application and may—
   (a) confirm the decision;
   (b) vary the decision;
   (c) suspend the decision; or
   (d) revoke the decision.

SECTION: 39

HEADING: Periodic review

1. Every declaration continuing for more than 12 months shall be reviewed by the regional council at least once in every 12-month period.
2. The council shall consider whether the declaration or restriction remains necessary.

SECTION: 40

HEADING: Revocation of declaration

1. A regional council shall revoke a declaration where it is satisfied that the circumstances giving rise to the declaration no longer exist.
2. The revocation shall be recorded in the register.

SECTION: 41

HEADING: Appeal to District Court

1. A person directly affected by a declaration or access restriction may appeal against the decision to the District Court.
2. An appeal shall be filed within 28 days after notice of the decision is received.
3. The filing of an appeal shall not, of itself, suspend the operation of the decision.

SECTION: 42

HEADING: Powers of District Court

On an appeal under section 41, the District Court may—

(a) confirm the decision;

(b) vary the decision;

(c) refer the matter back to the regional council; or

(d) make such other order as the Court considers just.

PARTNUMBER: 8

PARTTITLE: Offences

SECTION: 43

HEADING: Unlawful entry

1. Every person commits an offence who knowingly enters or remains in a place in contravention of an access restriction imposed under this Act.
2. A person convicted of an offence against subsection (1) is liable to a fine not exceeding $5,000.

AMENDMENT: Section 43(2): substituted, on 1 July 2019, by section 10 of the Harmful Places Amendment Act 2019 (2019 No 27).

SECTION: 44

HEADING: Failure to comply with direction

A person commits an offence who knowingly fails to comply with a lawful direction given by an authorised officer under this Act.

SECTION: 45

HEADING: Damage to warning signs and barriers

1. Every person commits an offence who intentionally or recklessly damages, removes, destroys, or interferes with any warning sign, fence, gate, or barrier established under this Act.
2. A person convicted of an offence against this section is liable to a fine not exceeding $10,000.

SECTION: 46

HEADING: False information

Every person commits an offence who knowingly gives false or misleading information to a regional council or authorised officer in connection with the administration of this Act.

SECTION: 47

HEADING: Failure to comply with notice

Every person commits an offence who fails, without reasonable excuse, to comply with a notice lawfully issued under this Act.

SECTION: 48

HEADING: Continuing offences

Where an offence against this Act is a continuing offence, the offender commits a further offence on each day during which the offence continues.

PARTNUMBER: 9

PARTTITLE: Management and Remediation

SECTION: 49

HEADING: Management plans

1. A regional council may prepare a management plan for any harmful place or dangerous place.
2. A management plan may provide for—
   (a) the removal of hazards;
   (b) restrictions on access;
   (c) monitoring;
   (d) inspections;
   (e) public warnings;
   (f) emergency procedures;
   (g) remediation; and
   (h) reopening of the place.

SECTION: 50

HEADING: Monitoring

A regional council may monitor a place subject to a declaration under this Act for the purpose of determining whether the relevant hazard has been reduced or removed.

SECTION: 51

HEADING: Remediation

1. A regional council may require the owner or occupier of a harmful place to undertake reasonable measures to remediate the place.
2. The measures may include—
   (a) removal of contaminated material;
   (b) demolition or repair of unsafe structures;
   (c) stabilisation of land;
   (d) removal of dangerous substances; and
   (e) restoration of safe access.

SECTION: 52

HEADING: Reopening of places

1. A regional council may permit a harmful place or dangerous place to be reopened where satisfied that the relevant risk has been adequately controlled.
2. The reopening may be subject to conditions.

SECTION: 53

HEADING: Temporary reopening

A regional council may temporarily reopen a restricted place for inspection, maintenance, remediation, scientific investigation, or any other purpose approved by the council.

PARTNUMBER: 10

PARTTITLE: Special Classes of Harmful Places

SECTION: 54

HEADING: Abandoned buildings

A regional council may declare an abandoned building or structure to be a harmful place where the condition of the building or structure creates a material risk to persons.

SECTION: 55

HEADING: Contaminated land

1. A place may be declared a harmful place where it is contaminated by any substance capable of causing harm.
2. A restriction under this section may prohibit or limit—
   (a) excavation;
   (b) removal of soil;
   (c) disturbance of the land;
   (d) access to water;
   (e) consumption of food produced at the place; or
   (f) any other activity likely to increase exposure.

SECTION: 56

HEADING: Dangerous natural places

1. A regional council may declare any area to be a dangerous place where a natural hazard presents a serious risk to persons.
2. Natural hazards include—
   (a) unstable cliffs;
   (b) landslides;
   (c) rockfalls;
   (d) unstable ground;
   (e) flooding;
   (f) dangerous waterways;
   (g) geothermal activity; and
   (h) coastal erosion.

SECTION: 57

HEADING: Industrial and commercial places

A place used for industrial or commercial purposes may be declared a harmful place where activities undertaken there create a material risk to persons outside the place.

SECTION: 58

HEADING: Places affected by fire

A building or other place affected by fire may be declared a dangerous place where the building, structure, smoke, substances, or other consequences of the fire present a serious risk.

SECTION: 59

HEADING: Dangerous substances

A place containing any substance which presents a serious risk to persons may be declared a dangerous place.

PARTNUMBER: 11

PARTTITLE: Administration

SECTION: 60

HEADING: Council policies

1. Every regional council shall establish procedures for the administration of this Act.
2. Such procedures shall include provision for—
   (a) the identification of hazards;
   (b) assessment of risks;
   (c) declarations;
   (d) access restrictions;
   (e) inspections;
   (f) enforcement; and
   (g) review of decisions.

SECTION: 61

HEADING: Cooperation between regional councils

Regional councils may cooperate with one another in relation to harmful places or dangerous places affecting more than one region.

SECTION: 62

HEADING: Cooperation with other authorities

A regional council may request information or assistance from any department of State, local authority, emergency service, or other public authority.

SECTION: 63

HEADING: Delegation

1. A regional council may delegate any of its functions or powers under this Act to—
   (a) a committee;
   (b) an employee;
   (c) an authorised officer; or
   (d) any other suitable person.
2. The council shall remain responsible for the proper exercise of delegated functions.

SECTION: 64

HEADING: Records

Every regional council shall keep proper records of declarations, restrictions, inspections, notices, reviews, and enforcement proceedings under this Act.

SECTION: 65

HEADING: Annual report

Every regional council shall include in its annual report a summary of the administration of this Act during the preceding year.

PARTNUMBER: 12

PARTTITLE: Miscellaneous

SECTION: 66

HEADING: Regulations

The Governor-General may, by Order in Council, make regulations for the purposes of this Act, including regulations—

(a) prescribing forms;

(b) prescribing fees;

(c) prescribing standards for warning signs;

(d) prescribing requirements relating to registers;

(e) prescribing procedures for applications and notices; and

(f) generally giving effect to this Act.

SECTION: 67

HEADING: Fees

1. A regional council may prescribe reasonable fees for applications, inspections, certificates, or other services provided under this Act.
2. No fee shall be imposed for an emergency inspection undertaken solely for the protection of the public.

AMENDMENT: Section 67: substituted, on 16 November 2012, by section 11 of the Harmful Places Amendment Act 2012 (2012 No 38).

SECTION: 68

HEADING: No compensation for restrictions

No person shall be entitled to compensation merely by reason of a restriction imposed under this Act.

SECTION: 69

HEADING: Protection of officers

No regional council, member of a regional council, officer, or authorised officer shall be personally liable for any act done in good faith in the exercise of a power under this Act.

SECTION: 70

HEADING: Application to Crown land

This Act applies to Crown land.

SECTION: 71

HEADING: Application to private land

This Act applies to private land.

SECTION: 72

HEADING: Saving of existing restrictions

Any lawful restriction applying to a place immediately before the commencement of this Act shall continue until revoked, replaced, or otherwise dealt with under this Act or any other enactment.

PARTNUMBER: 13

PARTTITLE: Transitional and Final Provisions

SECTION: 73

HEADING: Existing declarations

Any place declared dangerous or unsafe under an enactment in force immediately before the commencement of this Act may be treated as a harmful place or dangerous place under this Act.

SECTION: 74

HEADING: Existing notices

Any notice issued before the commencement of this Act in relation to a place which would have constituted a harmful place or dangerous place under this Act shall continue in force until reviewed by the appropriate regional council.

SECTION: 75

HEADING: Review of Act

1. The Minister shall commission a review of the operation of this Act not later than 5 years after its commencement.
2. The review shall consider—
   (a) the effectiveness of the Act;
   (b) the use of access restrictions;
   (c) enforcement;
   (d) public safety;
   (e) the effect on owners and occupiers; and
   (f) whether amendments are desirable.

SECTION: 76

HEADING: Annual review of long-term closures

1. A place which has been continuously closed under this Act for a period exceeding 3 years shall be subject to a special review.
2. The review shall determine whether—
   (a) the closure should continue;
   (b) the closure should be varied;
   (c) alternative measures are available; or
   (d) the place may safely be reopened.

AMENDMENT: Section 76: inserted, on 20 August 2020, by section 12 of the Harmful Places Amendment Act 2020 (2020 No 19).

SECTION: 77

HEADING: No duty to identify every harmful place

Nothing in this Act imposes a duty upon a regional council to identify every harmful place or dangerous place within its region.

SECTION: 78

HEADING: Emergency legislation

Nothing in this Act limits any power conferred under any enactment relating to emergency management, civil defence, public health, fire, or national security.

SECTION: 79

HEADING: Repeals and consequential amendments

The enactments specified in Schedule 1 are amended or repealed as provided in that Schedule.

SECTION: 80

HEADING: Savings

1. Nothing in this Act affects proceedings commenced before the commencement of this Act.
2. Proceedings commenced under any enactment repealed by this Act may be continued as if that enactment had not been repealed.

SECTION: 81

HEADING: Further review of restrictions

A regional council may review any declaration or restriction at any time where new information concerning the relevant hazard becomes available.

AMENDMENT: Section 81: inserted, on 4 April 2024, by section 6 of the Harmful Places Amendment Act 2024 (2024 No 11).

SECTION: 82

HEADING: Transitional provision relating to electronic notices

Nothing in section 18A affects the validity of a notice issued before the commencement of that section.

AMENDMENT: Section 82: inserted, on 17 September 2015, by section 9 of the Harmful Places Amendment Act 2015 (2015 No 42).
